# Telesat: a US$1.7 billion maturity on December 6, 2026, a crown-jewel LEO business moved 62% out of the lenders' reach, and a lender lawsuit racing the clock
As of: 2026-09-23. Slug: telesat. Deal type: live pre-restructuring — a first-lien maturity wall (US$1.71 billion due December 6, 2026, US$438 million more in 2027) at the legacy GEO satellite subsidiary; a contested September 2025 distribution of 62% of the Telesat Lightspeed (LEO) equity to a non-guarantor affiliate; term-lender lawsuits in New York and Ontario (fraudulent conveyance, oppression, "all or substantially all" covenant); an August 2026 lender recapitalisation proposal not yet agreed; going-concern doubt in the audited accounts; S&P at CC. ## 1. Situation summary and timeline

Telesat is Canada's satellite operator: a 13-satellite geostationary (GEO) fleet whose broadcast and enterprise revenue is shrinking fast, and a low-earth-orbit constellation, Telesat Lightspeed, being built with $2.54 billion of Canadian and Quebec government loans and now a $2.7 billion Canadian military contract. The two businesses sit in different legal boxes. All of the old debt — about US$2.1 billion of term loan and notes — sits at the GEO company, Telesat GEO Inc. (formerly Telesat Canada), and is guaranteed only by GEO entities. Lightspeed sits in "LEO Non-Guarantor" entities that owe the GEO lenders nothing. In September 2025 Telesat moved 62% of the equity of the LEO business from the GEO company up to a non-guarantor affiliate of the parent, leaving the GEO lenders with a claim on 38% [S1][S5]. Four months later the term lenders sued in two countries to unwind it [S1][S6]. The GEO company cannot repay US$1.71 billion due on December 6, 2026 from its own cash ($160.8 million at June 30, 2026, plus a US$120 million loan raised in August); the auditors have flagged substantial doubt about going concern; S&P says there is "certainty" the debt cannot be repaid as it comes due [S1][S3][S8][S22]. The lenders — Silver Point, Sound Point and GoldenTree lead the group — proposed in August a recapitalisation that gives them 100% of principal in new debt, collateralised by LEO equity, with cash from new junior capital; management says it wants "a consensual plan" and denies that Chapter 11 is being considered [S8][S12]. Eleven weeks remain.

## 2. Business overview and why it broke

Telesat Corporation (Ottawa) is the general partner of Telesat Partnership LP and owns Telesat GEO Inc. (formerly Telesat Canada) and, through the LEO Non-Guarantors (Telesat LEO Holding ULC, Lightspeed LEO Limited Partnership, Telesat LEO ULC and subsidiaries), the Lightspeed business [S1]. **GEO**: 13 geostationary satellites selling broadcast (direct-to-home television distribution, $196.2 million of 2025 revenue, −28.5%) and enterprise (data, government, maritime, aero) capacity; 2025 revenue $418 million, adjusted EBITDA $213 million (50.9% margin), GEO segment adjusted EBITDA $284 million (77%); 2026 GEO guidance $300–320 million of revenue and $210–230 million of adjusted EBITDA; fleet utilisation 60% at June 30, 2026 after retiring Telstar 14R and Anik F4; GEO backlog $0.8–0.9 billion [S1][S3][S4]. **LEO**: Telesat Lightspeed, 225 satellites (after the August expansion) built by MDA Space, first launches late 2026, global service targeted for the end of Q1 2028; cumulative investment about $2.7 billion to mid-2026 against a total build of about $7 billion; LEO backlog $5.6 billion pro forma for ESCP-P; customers include Viasat, Orange, Northwestel and Canada's Defence Investment Agency [S3][S7][S8].

**Causes of distress, in Moyer's categories.** (1) *Secular decline of the core business*: North American DTH television customers (Nimiq 4, Anik F3 contract expiries) are not renewing, S&P's stated driver at both downgrades; revenue has fallen from $571 million in 2024 to $418 million in 2025 to a $300–320 million run-rate in 2026, a halving in two years [S1][S20][S22]. (2) *A capital structure built for a different company*: the US$1.9 billion Term Loan B and notes were sized in 2019–21 against $700–800 million of EBITDA; against GEO's 2026 guidance the same debt is 12–14x [S1][S23]. (3) *A liquidity event that is a date, not a shock*: everything matures on December 6, 2026 and the revolver expired in 2024, so there is no bridge [S1]. (4) *The structural choice*: rather than let the LEO growth business carry the old debt, Telesat ring-fenced it — first by building it in non-guarantor entities with its own government financing, then by distributing 62% of its equity out of the GEO group — which is what turned a refinancing problem into a fight [S1][S5]. The sequence: creditors organise (April–July 2024) → Telesat buys back debt at 40–50 cents while it can (2024–25) → downgrades to CCC−/Caa2 (May–June 2025) → the 62% distribution (September 2025) → lawsuits (January 2026) → going-concern doubt and S&P's CC (March–May 2026) → US$120 million of new liquidity at yet another non-guarantor and the lender proposal (August 2026) → eleven weeks to maturity.

**The creditor's caveats.** First, consolidated numbers flatter nothing here — they *understate* GEO EBITDA (LEO start-up costs are expensed) but *overstate* what the lenders can reach, because LEO's cash and, after September 2025, 62% of its value are outside the guarantor group. The 20-F says the GEO company's own cash and cash flow "are not expected to be sufficient" for the December maturities [S1]. Second, the going-concern paragraph is in the audited statements and the auditors' report: "material uncertainty… casts substantial doubt as to Telesat Canada's ability to meet its obligations as they come due" [S1]. Third, the covenant EBITDA ($294 million) is $80 million above the IFRS adjusted figure because it excludes FX losses on US-dollar debt translated into Canadian dollars; the desk uses the GEO segment number [S1]. Fourth, the huge net losses are dominated by the mark-to-market of the government warrants — a liability that rises as Lightspeed's value rises — so a bigger loss is, perversely, good news for whoever ends up owning the LEO equity [S3][S8]. Fifth, the US$120 million August loan sits at a *non-guarantor* subsidiary of Telesat GEO, secured on that subsidiary's assets "and certain assets of Telesat GEO" — new secured debt arranged so the existing lenders' documents do not restrict it, which is the drop-down pattern from episodes 2 and 3 in miniature [S3][S26].

**Structural points.** The GEO lenders' collateral is "substantially all of Telesat Canada's assets, excluding assets relating to the Telesat Lightspeed business" [S1]; the 38% of LEO equity that stayed inside the guarantor group is the one route by which LEO value reaches them, and the 20-F says that if the lawsuit succeeds "this equity value would be subject to claims of Telesat Canada debtholders", and that pledging "some or all of the 62%" "may be required as a condition of any consensual refinancing" [S1]. Structural subordination runs both ways: GEO lenders are structurally junior to the government loans at LEO; the governments are structurally senior to everyone on the LEO assets and, through the warrants, own 11.87% of the upside. The three secured GEO instruments are pari passu first lien — no first-out/second-out tiering exists yet; the lender proposal would create new tiers. The 6.5% notes are the only contractually junior GEO claim. Trade, lease and litigation claims at GEO rank with the unsecured notes; the Brazilian tax dispute ($109.4 million alleged) and the Shaw contract dispute sit there too [S1].

## 5. The key documents and what they say

- **Credit Agreement (Mar 28, 2012, amended and restated Dec 6, 2019; seventh amendment May 9, 2023 for SOFR).** Parties: Telesat Canada and Telesat LLC as borrowers, the Guarantors, WSFS as administrative agent (the original agent was replaced; not sourced when). Terms per the 20-F: first-priority liens; total leverage ratio covenants; restrictions on liens, debt, investments, mergers, asset sales, dividends, sale-leasebacks, new subsidiaries, subordinated-debt repayment. **§6.03** is the provision the lenders sue on: the "all or substantially all" merger/asset-sale covenant — the plaintiffs say distributing 62% of Lightspeed "resulted in Telesat Canada transferring all or substantially all of its value" to SpinCo [S1]. The restricted-payments and investments baskets that Telesat says permitted the distribution are not quoted anywhere public; Telesat's position is that it was "accomplished in strict accordance with relevant debt agreements" [S6]. Defined terms confirm the design: "Unrestricted Subsidiary" as defined in the Credit Agreement and Indentures; "LEO Non-Guarantors" own all Lightspeed assets [S1].
- **The three indentures (2026 secured, 2027 secured, 2027 unsecured; Telesat Canada and Telesat LLC as issuers).** Covenants on debt, liens, dividends and restricted payments or investments, affiliate transactions, satellite insurance, asset sales, dividend blockers on subsidiaries, and "designate subsidiaries as unrestricted subsidiaries"; the secured notes share the first-priority liens with the term loan [S1].
- **The September 12, 2025 distribution.** Telesat Canada → Telesat LEO CanHold Corporation ("SpinCo"), 62% of the equity of the LEO Non-Guarantors; recipient "wholly-owned by Telesat Canada's parent entities and… a non-guarantor under Telesat Canada's debt documents"; stated purpose in the 20-F: "more flexibility to Telesat Corporation to raise capital in the future" [S1][S5]. Why 62% and not 100% is not explained publicly; the lawsuit implies the capacity was limited (a full transfer would plainly be "substantially all").
- **The statements of claim (Jan 21, 2026; not fetched, summarised in the 20-F).** Four theories: (i) CBCA ss. 34 or 36 — the statutory solvency tests a corporation must meet to pay a dividend or distribute assets; (ii) oppression — the transaction was "oppressive to, unfairly prejudicial to, or unfairly disregarded the reasonable expectations of the Term Lenders"; (iii) breach of Credit Agreement §6.03; (iv) Ontario Fraudulent Conveyances Act s. 2 — "Telesat Canada was insolvent at the time of the Transaction and undertook the Transaction with the intent to harm and prejudice the Term Lenders." Relief: an interest in Telesat Corporation, SpinCo or Telesat LEO Holdings "equal to the value of the Transferred Equity", damages, a declaration the transaction is void against the lenders, restoration under CBCA s. 118(2), costs. The Ontario action names the directors personally [S1]. Press adds the "crown-jewel asset" and "textbook fraud" characterisations and the MHR-benefit allegation [S9][S10].
- **The Telesat Lightspeed Financing (Sep 13, 2024; conditions precedent met Nov 15, 2024).** As above; note the covenant requiring Telesat LEO "to operate with a significant degree of independence from Telesat Canada's GEO business" and the ban on "financial assistance to any affiliates" other than LEO subsidiaries — the government lenders contractually fenced LEO cash away from GEO before Telesat moved the equity [S1].
- **The Telesat Canada Reorganization and Divestiture Act, S.C. 1991, c. 52, s. 8(2)**: "No Act relating to the solvency or winding-up of a corporation applies to Telesat and in no case shall the affairs of Telesat be wound up unless authorized by an Act of Parliament"; "Telesat" is Telesat Canada "and includes any successor by way of amalgamation" [S31]. Whether this shields the renamed Telesat GEO Inc. from a CCAA or Chapter 11 filing, or only from a winding-up, is untested; the Globe notes it "is unclear what that would mean for a subsidiary of the company, or a U.S.-based filing" [S12].
- **The August 2026 lender proposal (no document public).** Per the Globe: lenders receive 100% of principal in new debt, secured by LEO equity, with cash from new junior capital; no agreement [S12].
- **The US$120 million loan (Aug 12, 2026; agreement to be filed with the Q2 6-K).** Secured, four-year, SOFR-based, prepayment premium, at a non-guarantor subsidiary of Telesat GEO, "within existing covenant scope" per Goldberg [S3][S8][S26].

## 6. Where the fulcrum security sits and why

The arithmetic runs in two layers. **GEO alone**: US$1.93 billion of pari passu first-lien debt (term loan plus both secured notes) against GEO adjusted EBITDA guidance of $210–230 million (about US$150–165 million) — 12–13x — on a fleet whose revenue is falling 25% a year and which impaired $365 million of goodwill, slots and satellites in 2025 [S1][S4]. A shrinking GEO fleet does not carry a double-digit multiple (a desk assumption, not a sourced figure); on GEO alone the first-lien class is impaired and is the fulcrum, and the 6.5% unsecured notes would be near zero. **Add LEO**: the governments' 11.87% warrants are carried at more than $1.3 billion, which implies a fully diluted LEO equity value of roughly $11 billion (this research's division of the company's warrant value by the warrant percentage; the company has not stated a LEO valuation) [S8]. Even a fraction of that covers the GEO stack several times over. So the fulcrum is not a class; it is a *percentage*. With 38% of LEO inside the guarantor group, and at anything like the implied value, the secured debt is covered and the unsecured notes are money-good — which is what a 71-cent price on the 6.5% notes is saying [S24]. With the 62% gone for good and LEO valued conservatively, the first lien is the fulcrum. The lawsuit and the "LEO equity as collateral" term in the lender proposal are both attempts to move the fulcrum from the lenders' paper onto the LEO equity. Moyer's frame: the fulcrum is where enterprise value runs out; here the enterprise is being defined by litigation. The class that holds it: the term lenders (>75% in the ad hoc group led by Silver Point, Sound Point and GoldenTree) [S12][S17].

## 7. The restructuring, step by step (what has happened and what is proposed)

1. **Ring-fence the growth asset (2024).** Lightspeed is built in LEO Non-Guarantor entities; the governments lend $2.54 billion against LEO assets only, take 11.87% warrants, and require LEO's independence from GEO [S1][S29].
2. **Shrink the wall at a discount (2021–25).** US$857 million of GEO debt retired for US$450 million; the 2024 term-loan buyback at 50 cents, the 2025 notes at 40 [S1].
3. **Move the equity (Sep 12, 2025).** 62% of LEO's equity distributed from Telesat Canada to SpinCo, a non-guarantor; 38% left behind; stated purpose, flexibility to raise capital at the parent [S1][S5].
4. **Lenders sue (Jan 21, 2026)** in New York and Ontario on the four theories above; Telesat moves to dismiss in New York (Apr 2026) on jurisdiction [S1][S28].
5. **Auditors and agencies say the quiet part (Mar–May 2026).** Going-concern doubt; S&P to CC: "certainty" of non-repayment; only "a distressed restructuring" or a stop in debt service moves it to SD/D [S1][S22].
6. **Make LEO bigger and more valuable (Aug 4, 2026).** ESCP-P adds $2.3–2.7 billion of contracted revenue and 69 satellites; warrants revalued above $1.3 billion [S3][S7][S8].
7. **Add liquidity where the lenders cannot reach it (Aug 12, 2026).** US$120 million at a GEO non-guarantor, secured on that entity and "certain assets of Telesat GEO" [S3][S26].
8. **The lenders' proposal (Aug 2026)**: par recovery in new debt + a pledge of LEO equity + new junior capital for cash; not accepted [S12].
9. **What remains**: a consensual deal before December 6 (the 20-F itself anticipates a pledge of "some or all of the 62%" as the price [S1]); a forbearance; a default and acceleration; or a filing whose legal basis the 1991 Act complicates [S12][S31].

## 8. Who wins, who gets diluted or primed, and by how much

No plan, so no recovery table; the ranges below are this research's reading of prices and structure.

| Class | Claim | Where it stands | What decides it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| US$120m new secured loan (Aug 2026) | US$120m | Ahead of everyone on its borrower's assets and some GEO assets | Its collateral package (not public) |
| First-lien GEO debt (TLB, 2026 and 2027 secured notes) | US$1,932.5m | Impaired on GEO alone; covered if the 62% comes back or is pledged; prices not public | The lawsuit, the pledge, the LEO valuation |
| 6.5% unsecured notes | US$213.0m | ~71 cents: the market expects LEO value to reach GEO creditors | Same |
| Government LEO loans | $716m drawn of $2.54bn | Fully covered, senior on LEO, with an 11.87% warrant kicker | Lightspeed execution |
| Telesat Corporation equity (PSP, MHR, public) | — | Holds 62% of LEO through SpinCo plus 38% through GEO; TSX30-ranked stock | Whether the courts or a deal hand part of the 62% to the lenders |

**Where the value accumulated.** In Lightspeed, and specifically in the 62% of it that now sits at SpinCo, outside the GEO guarantor group: on the company's own warrant mark that slice is worth several times the entire GEO debt stack. The GEO lenders are left with a first lien on a shrinking fleet, 38% of LEO, and a lawsuit. The governments hold the safest paper in the group — first lien on LEO plus 11.87% of its equity. The equity holders, PSP and MHR, hold the growth asset with a public-company valuation that rose 37% on one contract [S1][S3][S8].

**Twists, turns and risks.** September 12, 2025: the distribution itself, done while the auditors were months from writing a going-concern paragraph and while S&P had already flagged "distressed exchange" risk — the insolvency-at-the-time question is the whole fraudulent-conveyance case. January 21, 2026: the lenders suing in two jurisdictions and naming the directors in Ontario. April 2026: the jurisdictional motion in New York, which could leave only the Ontario action. August 4 and 12, 2026: a $2.7 billion contract that made LEO more valuable and a US$120 million loan at another non-guarantor, in the same fortnight. December 6, 2026: the date.

**Winners, losers, and who was left in the dust (so far).** Winners: the governments of Canada and Quebec (first lien on LEO, warrants now worth over $1.3 billion on a $2.54 billion loan) and, on paper, the Telesat Corporation shareholders. Losers: the first-lien GEO lenders, who priced a 2019 loan against $700 million of EBITDA and now face 12–13x on GEO alone, with their claim on the growth asset cut from 100% to 38%. Left in the dust for now: any GEO creditor without a seat in the ad hoc group — and the 6.5% noteholders if the secured class settles for the LEO pledge and leaves them behind it.

## 10. How a special situations desk would look at each part of the structure

- **The term loan and 2026 secured notes.** Same lien, same date, same fate. You own them to own the litigation: the value of the claim is the value of the 38% you have plus the probability-weighted value of the 62% you might get back or get pledged. The desk's model is a decision tree — settlement with a LEO pledge (the lenders' proposal), a court win (voiding the transfer or damages equal to the Transferred Equity), a court loss (GEO-only recovery), a filing — and the trade is the price of the loan against that tree. The group already holds >75%, so the marginal buyer is buying a seat at a table that is set [S1][S12][S17].
- **The 4.875% 2027 secured notes.** Same lien, six months later; whatever happens on December 6 happens to them by acceleration or by deal.
- **The 6.5% 2027 unsecured notes at ~71.** The cheapest way to express "LEO value reaches GEO creditors": if the secured class is covered by a LEO pledge, the unsecureds are money-good behind them; if the secured class is the fulcrum, these are the option that goes to zero. 71 says the market leans to the first [S24].
- **The US$120 million August loan.** Not tradable publicly; interesting only as a signal that the company can still create senior, separately secured liquidity outside the lenders' documents — and as the next thing the lenders will ask to see.
- **The government loans.** Not a trade; the reference point. Their independence and no-financial-assistance covenants mean LEO cash cannot rescue GEO, which is why the GEO refinancing must be solved with LEO *equity*, not LEO cash [S1].
- **The equity (TSAT).** A public proxy for the 62%: it fell 21% on the lawsuit, rose 37% on ESCP-P, fell 13% on the US$120 million loan day. A desk that owns the loan and is short the equity is expressing "the 62% comes back"; the reverse expresses "the ring-fence holds" [S3][S10][S16].
- **Relative value / history.** The company bought back US$857 million of this debt at an average of 52.5 cents; the 2024 term-loan buyback at 50 tells you where the loan traded before the ring-fence was completed [S1].

## 11. Risks and what could derail it

1. **The date.** December 6, 2026: without a deal, forbearance or filing, US$1.71 billion defaults; cross-default takes the 2027 notes with it [S1].
2. **The lawsuits.** A New York dismissal on jurisdiction narrows the fight to Ontario; a loss for the lenders leaves GEO-only recoveries; a win could void the transfer or award damages equal to the Transferred Equity. No ruling is public [S1][S28].
3. **The 1991 Act.** Section 8(2) may bar a winding-up of Telesat GEO without an Act of Parliament and casts doubt on the availability of ordinary insolvency law; whether it blocks a CCAA or Chapter 11 filing, protects a renamed successor, or reaches a non-Canadian filing is untested [S12][S31].
4. **Governments as gatekeepers.** The LEO loans' change-of-control, independence and financial-assistance covenants mean any pledge of LEO equity to GEO lenders, or any change in who owns LEO, likely needs the governments' consent [S1].
5. **LEO execution.** First launches late 2026, service Q1 2028, $1.3–1.5 billion of 2026 spend; a slip cuts the value everyone is fighting over [S3][S8].
6. **GEO decline.** Revenue −25% a year, utilisation 60%; the collateral shrinks while the parties argue [S3].
7. **Holdouts and the unsecured.** A secured-only deal that pledges LEO equity to the first lien primes the 6.5% notes; a deal that needs 100% of the term loan invites holdouts [S1][S12].
8. **Information gaps.** No public price on the loan, no term sheet for the proposal, no docket.

## 12. Concepts to teach in this episode

New (to add to the ledger as `introduced`, episode 5):

- `restricted_payments` (level 3, legal): the covenant that limits dividends, distributions and investments out of the credit group, and the baskets that permit them; the 62% distribution was made under (Telesat says) this capacity, and the lenders say the capacity did not exist. Moyer: covenants and the protection of the credit group.
- `fraudulent_conveyance` (level 3, legal): a transfer made while insolvent, or with intent to hinder creditors, can be unwound; Ontario Fraudulent Conveyances Act s. 2 and CBCA ss. 34/36 solvency tests for distributions; here, whether Telesat Canada was insolvent on September 12, 2025. Moyer: avoidance actions and the zone of insolvency.
- `all_or_substantially_all` (level 4, legal): the merger/asset-sale covenant (Credit Agreement §6.03) that bars transferring "all or substantially all" assets without lender consent; the plaintiffs argue 62% of LEO was substantially all of Telesat Canada's *value*. Not in Moyer by name.
- `oppression_remedy` (level 4, legal): Canada's statutory remedy for conduct that is oppressive or unfairly prejudicial to a stakeholder's reasonable expectations, available to creditors; no US equivalent; the second count in Ontario. Not in Moyer.
- `going_concern_doubt` (level 2, accounting): the auditor's and management's "substantial doubt" language and what triggers it; the 20-F paragraph tied "solely to the upcoming Telesat Canada Debt maturities in December 2026". Moyer: financial statement warning signs.
- `zone_of_insolvency` (level 4, legal): directors' duties when a company is near insolvency, and why creditors name directors personally (the Ontario action) and allege conflicted-interest benefit (the MHR claim). Moyer: governance in distress.
- `statutory_insolvency_bar` (level 4, legal): a special statute (the 1991 Act, s. 8(2)) that removes a company from ordinary solvency and winding-up law; a rare wrinkle that changes the lenders' leverage. Not in Moyer.
- `collateral_enhancement` (level 3, capital_structure): the price of an extension — lenders demanding a pledge of assets or equity that was outside their collateral (here, LEO equity) in exchange for extending or refinancing; the core of the August proposal. Moyer: out-of-court restructuring terms.
- `ccaa` (level 3, process): the Companies' Creditors Arrangement Act, Canada's court-supervised restructuring statute and the counterpart to Chapter 11, with a monitor rather than a debtor-in-possession model; the venue question if Telesat GEO files. Moyer: out-of-court vs in-court (Canadian analogue).

Reminders (status `introduced`; one clause on air): `unrestricted_subsidiary` (episode 3), `drop_down` (episode 2; the LEO ring-fence and the US$120 million loan at a non-guarantor are the pattern), `collateral_pools` and `non_recourse_debt` (episode 2; the government loans), `guarantee` and `structural_subordination` (episode 1; guarantor vs non-guarantor entities is the whole case), `maturity_wall` (episode 3), `interest_coverage` (episode 4; S&P's 0.7x), `secured_vs_unsecured`, `pari_passu`, `term_loan`, `revolver` (expired), `ad_hoc_group`, `holdout`, `fulcrum_security`, `refinancing`, `adjusted_ebitda`, `price_in_points`, `sponsor_option_value` (episode 4; the equity's incentive to keep LEO out of reach), `cooperation_agreement` (episode 4; the >75% group), `new_money_tranche` (episode 4; "cash from new junior capital"), `dilution`.

## 13. Glossary

- **All or substantially all**: a covenant threshold barring transfer of all or substantially all of a company's assets (measured, the lenders argue, by value) without consent.
- **CBCA sections 34 and 36**: the Canada Business Corporations Act solvency tests a corporation must meet before a distribution or share repurchase.
- **CCAA**: the Companies' Creditors Arrangement Act, Canada's court-supervised restructuring regime for large debtors.
- **CORRA**: the Canadian Overnight Repo Rate Average, the Canadian-dollar benchmark rate.
- **Collateral enhancement**: adding assets or equity to a lender's security package as consideration for an extension or amendment.
- **Distressed exchange**: an exchange in which holders receive less than the original promise and which the issuer would otherwise not honour; a default for rating agencies.
- **Drop-down**: moving assets to a subsidiary outside the credit group so they no longer secure or support the existing debt.
- **ESCP-P**: Enhanced Satellite Communications Project – Polar, the Canadian Armed Forces' Arctic satcom programme.
- **Fraudulent conveyance**: a transfer of property made to defeat creditors or while insolvent, which a court can set aside.
- **GEO / LEO**: geostationary orbit (a few large satellites, fixed position) / low earth orbit (a constellation of many small satellites).
- **Going-concern doubt**: an auditor's or management's statement that there is substantial doubt the company can meet its obligations for the next year.
- **Guarantor / non-guarantor**: an entity that does (or does not) guarantee the debt; only guarantors' assets and cash support the lenders' claims.
- **Oppression remedy**: a Canadian statutory remedy for corporate conduct that is oppressive, unfairly prejudicial to, or unfairly disregards the interests of a security holder or creditor.
- **Restricted payments**: dividends, distributions, investments and similar transfers out of the credit group, limited by covenant and permitted only within baskets.
- **Ring-fence**: the legal separation of a business so that its assets and cash are not available to the creditors of an affiliate.
- **SpinCo**: Telesat LEO CanHold Corporation, the non-guarantor recipient of the 62%.
- **Statutory insolvency bar**: a special Act providing that ordinary solvency and winding-up laws do not apply to a named corporation.
- **Warrant**: the right to buy shares at a set price; here, the governments' right to 11.87% of Telesat LEO.
- **Zone of insolvency**: the period when a company is insolvent or nearly so, during which directors' decisions are scrutinised for their effect on creditors.
