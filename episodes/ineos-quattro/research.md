# INEOS Quattro: a stressed credit before the restructuring — reading a €7 billion single-class secured stack, a 2027 maturity, and a founder writing cheques
As of: 2026-09-19. Slug: ineos-quattro. Deal type: live stressed credit — no restructuring proposed; liquidity management (shareholder equity, inventory monetisation, receivables securitisation, asset sales, buybacks) ahead of January 2027 maturities and a March 2029 maturity wall; watched by the market as a liability-management candidate. Framing: live.

## 1. Situation summary and timeline

INEOS Quattro is the styrenics, PVC, aromatics and acetyls arm of Sir Jim Ratcliffe's INEOS. It carries about €7.2 billion of gross debt, all of it senior secured and all of it ranking pari passu, against earnings that fell to €717 million in 2025 and rebounded in the first half of 2026 on a war-driven supply shock. Its bonds traded into the 70s in late 2025 and early 2026 as two rating agencies cut it into single-B territory; its founder responded with €200 million of new equity, a €300 million inventory-monetisation facility from Goldman Sachs, asset sales, and — to creditors' irritation — €400 million of purchases of listed chemical-company shares. Nothing has been restructured. The question the desk is asking is whether the group can carry a €3.9 billion March 2029 maturity through a European chemicals downturn, and what its owner will do if it cannot.

## 2. Business overview and why it broke

INEOS Quattro Holdings Limited is a private company registered in England, owned through INEOS Industries Holdings Limited by INEOS Limited (Isle of Man), which is owned by Jim Ratcliffe, Andrew Currie and John Reece; the directors regard Ratcliffe as the ultimate controlling party "by virtue of his majority shareholding" [S2]. It has four segments, run as two pairs since November 2025 [S1][S2]:

- **Styrolution**: styrene monomer, polystyrene, ABS and specialty styrenics; sites including Antwerp (Europe's largest chemical site), Bayport, Texas (the world's largest single-line styrene plant), and a 600-kilotonne ABS plant in Ningbo, China through a Sinopec joint venture held in an *unrestricted* subsidiary. Revenue 2025 €3,919 million (−17.5%); adjusted EBITDA €285 million [S2].
- **Inovyn**: Europe's largest PVC producer plus caustic soda, chlorine and derivatives; about 2,000 customers, 60–70% exposed to building and construction; Tavaux, France depends on nuclear power whose regulated ARENH pricing ended in 2025. 2025 EBITDA €212 million (2024: €348 million) [S2][S5][S14].
- **Acetyls**: acetic acid, acetic anhydride, methanol, vinyl acetate (ex-BP). 2025 EBITDA €220 million, helped by an $90 million supplier settlement in February 2025 [S1][S5].
- **Aromatics**: paraxylene, PTA, benzene (ex-BP), hit by Chinese PTA oversupply. 2025 EBITDA roughly nil (2024: €47 million) [S5][S14].

Group revenue was €10,696 million in 2025, 47% Europe, 25% North America, 28% Asia, down 15.4% from €12,646 million on lower volumes and prices [S2]. Adjusted EBITDA fell from €1.4–1.5 billion at the 2021–22 peak (blog figure [S14]) to €912 million in 2024 and €717 million in 2025 [S2][S5]; the fourth quarter of 2025 produced only €77 million [S5].

**Causes of distress, in Moyer's categories.** This is a cyclical-shock case layered on acquisition leverage, not a fraud or a single liquidity event. (1) *Acquisition leverage*: the group was assembled with debt — Styrolution bought from BASF, Inovyn from the Solvay joint venture, and in 2021 BP's aromatics and acetyls for $5 billion, financed by €1.5 billion and $2 billion of term loans and €1.8 billion-equivalent of notes [S27][S29]. Ratcliffe's whole career is buying "unfashionable, unsexy" commodity chemical assets with borrowed money — Inspec in 1992, BP's Antwerp ethylene-oxide plant for £84 million in 1998, ICI's commodity chemicals in 2001, Innovene for $9 billion in 2005 [S27]. (2) *Secular pressure disguised as a cycle*: European chemicals face energy prices "around four times higher than in the United States" (Ratcliffe, February 2026 [S23]), carbon costs, and Chinese overcapacity; Europe's share of global chemical sales halved from 28% in the early 2000s to 13% by 2023 [S19]. INEOS has filed ten EU anti-dumping cases covering PVC, ABS, glycols and polyolefins [S21]. (3) *Commodity product mix*: unlike INEOS Group Holdings' ethane-advantaged crackers, Quattro's products are "more commodity-grade with less pricing power" and 34% of revenue is in Asia, where Chinese capacity sets the price (blog view [S14]). (4) *Fixed-cost operating leverage*: the response has been closures — Sarnia styrene (closed by June 2026), Texas City PX2, Tavaux chloromethanes, Rheinberg units, Channahon polystyrene — each with cash restructuring costs before any saving [S1][S2][S21].

The sequence: a strong 2021–22, a 2023–25 downturn that took EBITDA down 60–70% from peak while gross debt stayed above €7 billion, two 2025 downgrades that pushed bonds to ~80 and then ~70, a January 2026 liquidity package, and then a war-driven margin spike in Q2 2026 that lifted EBITDA to €407 million in a quarter and cut reported leverage from 7.7x to 6.4x — a spike management itself attributes to Middle East supply constraints, not to any change in the structural picture [S3][S5][S14].

**The creditor's caveats.**

- *Adjusted EBITDA is before "exceptional" items and after joint-venture add-backs.* The reconciliation in the Q2 report shows what sits below it: depreciation and impairment of €320.7 million in H1 2026, €28.1 million of exceptional impairments, a €54.9 million fair-value loss on the equity basket, and the group's share of JV depreciation (€48.6 million) [S1]. - *Inventory gains flatter the Q2 2026 spike.* Styrolution's €170 million Q2 EBITDA includes €70 million of inventory holding gains as feedstock prices rose (Q2 2025: a €38 million loss); Aromatics had €3 million of losses [S3]. FY2025 Styrolution EBITDA had absorbed €58.7 million of inventory losses and €21.1 million of one-off carbon-credit sales [S2]. Strip the inventory swing and the underlying Q2 improvement is roughly half the headline.
- *LTM EBITDA at June 2026 is about €883 million* (FY2025 €717.1 million less H1 2025 €462.9 million plus H1 2026 €628.9 million) [S1][S2]; net debt of €5,637 million on that is 6.4x, matching the company's figure. On FY2025 EBITDA of €717 million it is 7.9x; on Q4 2025 annualised (€308 million) it is over 18x. Gross debt of €7,212 million is 8.2x LTM. Moody's gross-leverage figure of 11.7x, cited by a credit blog, is on a lower, agency-adjusted 2025 base [S14].
- *Cash interest is the whole story.* €557.6 million of interest was paid in 2025 against €717 million of EBITDA and €239 million of capex; EBITDA minus capex minus interest was roughly minus €80 million before working capital, tax and restructuring cash. Operating cash flow of €757 million looks healthy only because €232 million came from working capital shrinking with volumes [S2]. Moody's, per the blog, put EBITDA-less-capex-to-interest cover at about 0.4x and projected negative free cash flow of about €100 million a year in 2025–26 [S14].
- *Net debt rose in H1 2026 despite €200 million of equity and €187 million of inventory-facility drawings.* Net debt went from €5,524 million to €5,637 million while the group prepaid €242 million of 2027 debt, paid €265 million of interest, spent €200 million on chemical shares and €215 million on investing activities overall [S1]. The equity and the Goldman money did not reduce leverage; they funded the year.
- *Liquidity is real but partly borrowed against the collateral.* Cash of €1,576 million plus €547 million of undrawn receivables capacity plus €160 million of local bank lines gives about €2.3 billion of liquidity at June 30, 2026, against €697 million maturing in January 2027 [S1]. But €190.6 million of that cash came from selling inventory to Goldman with an obligation to buy it back, and the receivables programmes sell trade receivables into vehicles secured on those receivables — neither is available to noteholders in a downside [S1].
- *Going concern.* The directors state the group has "sufficient cashflows to meet its working capital requirements for the next twelve months" after stress-testing, with net assets of €1,818.2 million at June 30, 2026 [S1]. Twelve months from July 2026 does not reach March 2029.

**Structural points.**

- *One class.* Since January 15, 2025 there is no unsecured or subordinated debt. The 2027, 2029 and 2030 notes and the 2014 and 2020 term-loan facilities are "Pari Passu Debt" under an intercreditor agreement dated November 7, 2014, amended and restated January 31, 2020, with HSBC Corporate Trustee Company as security agent; each notes trustee acceded as a "Pari Passu Debt Representative" [S2]. The 2030 notes "rank pari passu in right of payment with... indebtedness of IQF2 under the Credit Facility Agreements, the 2029 Senior Secured Notes, the 2027 Senior Secured Notes and certain hedging obligations" [S2]. A restructuring would therefore have to divide one class of about €6.8 billion of like-ranking lenders — which is precisely the situation in which a company can offer some of them a better place in line than others.
- *Collateral.* Share pledges over guarantor subsidiaries in Belgium, England, France (INOVYN France and INOVYN Oléfines France), Germany, Hong Kong, Mexico, Norway, Singapore, South Korea, Sweden, Switzerland and the US; debentures and asset security in England, Hong Kong, Singapore and the US; German fixed movable assets and intercompany receivables; a Swedish mortgage certificate; US UCC all-asset security, IP and a mortgage. Metals hedges and cash management share the collateral [S2].
- *Guarantor coverage.* Borrowers and guarantors must represent at least 85% of consolidated adjusted EBITDA and 85% of total assets, tested 150 days after year-end; INEOS Styrolution Canada was released as a guarantor on January 28, 2026 after Sarnia closed [S2].
- *What sits outside.* The Sinopec joint ventures (including the 600 kt Ningbo ABS plant and a third JV funded with €114.4 million of Sinopec proceeds in 2024) are held through *unrestricted subsidiaries* "not bound by the covenants... including with respect to limitations on indebtedness and restricted payments" [S2]. Receivables sold into the securitisations and inventory sold to Goldman are outside the collateral. Local bank lines sit at operating subsidiaries. The notes are "structurally subordinated to all liabilities (including trade payables) and preferred stock of each subsidiary... that is not a guarantor" [S2].
- *Covenant capacity.* The annual report lists the negative covenants (debt, restricted payments, investments, liens, asset sales, affiliate transactions, designation of unrestricted subsidiaries) but not the basket sizes [S2]. A credit blog asserts €1.75 billion of incremental debt capacity, "€2.5bn+" of permitted transfers to unrestricted subsidiaries, a 1.75x leverage-based test, and an ability to sell Inovyn if leverage is brought to 2.5x — these are the blog's readings of the indenture and are *not verified* against the document here [S14]. The term-loan asset-sale sweep (100% of net proceeds from material dispositions "subject to reinvestment rights") is in the annual report [S2].
- *No maintenance covenants* anywhere in the stack [S1][S2]: the lenders cannot call a default on leverage; only a payment default, a covenant breach or a maturity gives them a seat at the table.

## 5. The key documents and what they say

**Indentures (2027 notes, Styrolution GmbH issuer, Jan 31, 2020; 2029 and 2030 notes, INEOS Quattro Finance 2 plc issuer, Nov 14, 2023 and Oct 7, 2024; trustee HSBC Corporate Trustee Company (UK)).** Ranking language, quoted above: senior secured, pari passu with the credit facilities and the other notes, "effectively senior" to unsecured or junior-lien debt "to the extent of the value of the collateral", "effectively subordinated" to debt secured on assets that do not secure the notes and to all liabilities of non-guarantor subsidiaries [S2]. Listed on the Luxembourg Euro MTF [S1].

**2014 Term Loan Facilities Agreement (2027 term loans; borrowers INEOS Styrolution US Holding LLC and INEOS Styrolution Group GmbH; agent Barclays).** Margin 2.00% over Term SOFR + 0.10% CSA or EURIBOR with a 0.50% floor; bullet January 31, 2027; mandatory prepayment from 50% of excess cash flow (25% at ≤3.0x, 0% at ≤2.5x consolidated total net leverage), 100% of material asset-sale proceeds subject to reinvestment rights, and 100% of non-permitted debt proceeds; same collateral and covenants as the notes; "does not contain any financial maintenance covenants" [S2].

**2020 Term Loan Facilities Agreement (2029, 2030, 2031 term loans).** Margins 4.25%/4.50% (2029), 3.75%/4.00% (2030), 4.25% (2031); 0% floors; 0.25% quarterly amortisation on dollar tranches; bullets March 31, 2029, March 14, 2030, October 7, 2031; ECF sweep 50% stepping to 25% at ≤3.75x and 0% at ≤3.25x; 100% asset-sale and debt sweeps applied ratably across classes; guarantor coverage 85%/85%; the same negative covenants [S1][S2].

**Intercreditor Agreement (Nov 7, 2014, restated Jan 31, 2020; security agent HSBC).** Governs the 2014 lenders, hedge counterparties, each notes trustee and the 2020 agent as Pari Passu Debt Representatives, any future "Second Lien Debt Creditors", cash-management providers, intra-group creditors and "certain subordinated creditors"; establishes the Pari Passu Debt class and the (empty) second-lien class behind it [S2]. The release and enforcement provisions were not in the extract read.

**Inventory Monetisation Agreements (Goldman Sachs International, January 2026).** Up to €300 million; two years to January 2028, extendable by mutual agreement; the group "sells certain inventory to GSI and agrees to buy-back equivalent inventory at the end of the term at the same price"; GSI provides just-in-time use of the inventory for a fee, "subject to certain covenants and rights of GSI"; supported by a parent-company guarantee and a cash-collateral mechanism; carried as a €190.6 million borrowing at EURIBOR/SOFR + 4.50% [S1].

**Receivables Securitisation Programmes (Styrolution €550 million; Inovyn €240 million).** Mature January 2029 after the January 2026 extension; secured by pledges over receivables sold in; drawn cost is the lenders' commercial-paper cost plus 1.10%; 0.6% undrawn; capacity adjusts with eligible receivables [S1][S5].

**Not on the record.** No restructuring support agreement, no exchange offer, no forbearance, no DIP, no plan. The annual report's own risk factor is the closest thing to a term sheet: the principal shareholders "may have an interest in pursuing acquisitions, divestitures or other transactions, including repurchases of our debt, on the open market or otherwise, that... could enhance their equity investment, even though these transactions might involve risks to you" [S2].

## 6. Where the fulcrum security sits and why

There is no impaired class today; every instrument is current and the January 2027 maturities are covered by cash. The fulcrum question is hypothetical but the market has been pricing it, so the math matters.

**The claims.** Gross funded debt €7,212 million, of which €6,831 million is the single pari passu secured class (notes €2,117 million, term loans €4,524 million, 2027 paper €697 million) and €381 million is the structurally senior inventory and lease-adjacent layer; leases €317 million on top. Net of €1,576 million cash: €5,637 million [S1].

**The value, three ways.**

| Basis | EBITDA | Multiple | Enterprise value | Covers net debt of €5.6bn? | Where value runs out |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Trough, distressed | FY2025 €717m | 5x (distressed chemicals, blog range 4–5x [S14]) | €3.6bn | No, by €2.0bn | inside the pari passu secured class: ~55–65 cents on the €6.8bn |
| LTM June 2026 | €883m | 6.5x (S&P's threshold for the rating, press [S13]) | €5.7bn | Just | the secured class is roughly whole; equity ~nil |
| Mid-cycle | €1.1–1.4bn (blog [S14]) | 8x | €8.8–11bn | Yes, by €3–5bn | equity, comfortably |

The bonds at 70–80 in early 2026 [S13][S14][S15] were the market voting for the first row: a 70 price on a pari passu secured note is a statement that, at a distressed multiple on trough earnings, enterprise value runs out inside the secured class, so the *notes and the term loans together are the fulcrum* and Ratcliffe's equity is worth an option. The Q2 2026 EBITDA of €407 million, if it held for a year, would be €1.6 billion and put the fulcrum firmly in the equity; the company itself says the quarter was driven by a war-induced supply shock and inventory gains [S3]. The fulcrum, in other words, sits wherever the listener's view of mid-cycle EBITDA and the exit multiple puts it — Moyer's valuation chapter is the whole case.

**Who holds it.** The pari passu class is held by leveraged-loan investors and CLOs (term loans) and high-yield funds (notes); press reports "Wall Street hedge funds", funds "linked to Elliott Management", and short sellers active since late 2025, and a JNL/Jackson fund filing shows retail-fund ownership of the notes, but no organised group, steering committee or adviser mandate has been identified [S19][S22]. On the other side of the table sits one man with a majority stake and a record of writing cheques: €200 million in March 2026 and, with his co-owners, €400 million into peers' shares [S1][S26].

## 7. The restructuring, step by step

Nothing has been proposed. What has actually happened is a *liquidity campaign*, and the desk reads each step as a signal of what the owner is willing to do — in order:

1. **Buy back the front end at small discounts (May 2025 – June 2026).** €2.9 million and €16.4 million of 2027 notes at ~97–98; €19.6 million of the 2027 Euro TLB at 97.4; $190.6 million of the 2027 Dollar TLB in full in February 2026; €32.0 million of 2027 notes at 98.4 in April; €50.0 million of the Euro TLB in June. Total 2027 paper cut from €1,171 million at end-2024 to €697 million [S1][S2]. The message: the January 2027 maturity is being paid, not extended.
2. **Extend the receivables securitisation (January 2026)** from 2026 to January 2029, €790 million of capacity [S5].
3. **Sell inventory to Goldman (January 2026)**: €300 million of two-year money at E/S + 4.50% plus fees, secured by the inventory itself through a true sale and a parent guarantee; €190.6 million drawn by June [S1]. This is new money that ranks ahead of the notes on the assets it touches, without a lien, a consent or a covenant test — the sale-and-repurchase cousin of the SPV on-loan trick from the Saks episode.
4. **Take €200 million of equity from the shareholders (committed January, received March 2026)** as new shares [S1][S5]. Equity is the one source of cash that dilutes nobody but the owner and primes nobody.
5. **Announce asset sales (February–April 2026)**: preliminary talks on Inovyn chlor-vinyl assets worth "hundreds of millions of pounds" (FT, press) [S24]; the Italian chlor-alkali sale to Esseco at an undisclosed price [S1][S6]; Wilhelmshaven land for €41.8 million [S1]. Under the term loans, 100% of material asset-sale proceeds must prepay debt unless reinvested [S2].
6. **Close capacity (2024–26)**: Sarnia, Texas City PX2, Tavaux chloromethanes, Rheinberg units, Channahon [S1][S2][S21].
7. **Buy chemical stocks (April and August 2026)**: €199.7 million into "a basket of publicly traded liquid equities related to the chemical industry", marked down €54.9 million by June, followed by a reported second €200 million; creditors reportedly object that the cash should have retired discounted bonds [S1][S26].

What has *not* happened: no new Quattro bond or loan in 2026 while the sibling INEOS Group Holdings placed €700 million of 7⅞% 2031 notes in May and ~€742 million of term loans in June to clear its own 2027–28 maturities [S7][S8]. Quattro's market access for the €3.9 billion 2029 wall is the open question.

## 8. Who wins, who gets diluted or primed, and by how much

Because nothing has been restructured, the table below is what the *documents and the market* imply, not a plan.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Goldman inventory monetisation | 190.6 drawn / 300 capacity | New money, true-sale seniority over the inventory, parent guarantee, 4.50% margin plus fees | not traded |
| Receivables securitisations | 0 drawn / 790 | Extended to 2029; senior to the notes on receivables | not traded |

**Diluted or primed.** Nobody has been primed by a lien: the intercreditor's pari passu class is intact. But the 2029–31 holders have been *layered*: about €190 million of inventory money and up to €790 million of receivables money now sit ahead of them on the most liquid assets, and every buyback of 2027 paper at 97–98 spent collateral cash on the class that was already going to be paid first. The equity was not diluted because there is only one shareholder group; it was topped up.

**Where the value accumulated.** Through the downturn the enterprise value has been pooling in the same place it always was — the single secured class — while shrinking underneath it: on FY2025 earnings at a distressed multiple the class covers itself at roughly 55–65 cents, and the equity is an option on a chemicals recovery. The structurally senior layers (Goldman, the securitisation banks, local lenders) hold small, well-covered claims on the best assets. The owner has put value *in* rather than taken it out: €200 million of equity, two years of margin on inventory, and a reputational stake in not being the man who let INEOS go. The Q2 2026 war windfall accrued, for now, to cash on the balance sheet — and €200 million of it to a portfolio of other companies' shares.

**Twists, turns and risks.** Three moments changed the picture. November 13, 2025: the FT reported the bonds had fallen from par to 80 in a week and an investor floated a "Drahi-style" restructuring — the market's first open pricing of a liability-management outcome [S20]. January 30–31, 2026: the €500 million package and the 2027 notes' jump to 98, which turned the 2027 maturity from a catalyst into a non-event and moved the fight to 2029 [S5][S14]. March–June 2026: the Iran war, the closure of the Strait of Hormuz and a €407 million quarter that cut leverage from 7.7x to 6.4x on earnings the company itself calls supply-shock-driven [S3][S18]. The risk that remains is the same one Moyer would name: a multiple. If mid-cycle EBITDA is €1.1 billion the class is whole; if the trough is the new mid-cycle, the wall in March 2029 becomes the restructuring.

**Winners, losers, and who was left in the dust.** So far the winners are whoever bought the 2027 notes in the 90s in late 2025 and got 98 two months later, the 2030 noteholders who bought at 70 and saw 78 within weeks, and Goldman, which lent two-year money at 4.50% over base plus fees against inventory it legally owns. Ratcliffe's equity is the biggest loser on paper — he has put €200 million into a company whose debt the market said was worth 70–80 cents — and the biggest potential winner if the cycle turns, which is why he is buying peers' shares. The 2029 term-loan lenders, €2.8 billion of them, are the class with the least that has changed and the most at stake. Nobody has been left in the dust yet; the desk's job is to decide who would be.

## 9. Cast of characters: who's driving it, and what they're known for

**Sir Jim Ratcliffe (73), chairman of INEOS since 1998 and majority owner through INEOS Limited [S2].** Started at Exxon Chemicals and Courtaulds, MBA at London Business School, then Advent International and Inspec, where he led the 1992 buyout; left in 1998 to buy BP's Antwerp ethylene-oxide plant for £84 million with borrowed money and never stopped: ICI's commodity chemicals in 2001, BP's Innovene for $9 billion in 2005 ("quadrupled INEOS's turnover"), BP's aromatics and acetyls for $5 billion in 2021 [S2][S27]. The FT calls him the "new king of the high-yield market"; a Bloomberg columnist described "an eye for unloved commodity chemicals assets and an unquenchable thirst for debt" [S20] (search excerpt of a Bloomberg opinion piece). His own rule, per a business-buyers' profile: "the more deals you look at, the better deals you do" [S27]. He has been here before: in 2008, three years after the $9 billion Innovene loan, INEOS breached its covenants and won a reset from 230 banks in July 2009 by paying a consent fee, a higher margin and a EURIBOR floor [S9][S10]. He is a co-owner of Manchester United, closed the Grangemouth refinery in 2025 with 400 job losses while blaming carbon taxes and energy costs, and told a February 2026 industry summit that European energy prices are "unsurvivable for Europe's chemical industry" [S13][S23][S28]. ## 10. How a special situations desk would look at each part of the structure

- **2027 notes and Euro TLB (€697 million, four months to maturity).** Money-good on the numbers: €1,576 million of cash against €697 million due, an explicit statement from management, and the company buying the paper at 98 [S1][S3][S5]. The trade already happened — 95 to 98 on the January package. What is left is a carry trade: 2.25% coupon plus two points of pull-to-par over four months, against the tail risk that something breaks before January. A desk owns this only as a low-risk park or as a way to be in the room.
- **2029 notes (€1,125 million, 8.5% and 9.625%).** The instrument that expresses the view. At ~80 the yield is ~16% for a first-lien claim on a company that just printed a €407 million quarter; at a 5x trough multiple the same claim is worth 55–65 [S14]. The asymmetry is a valuation call on European chemicals plus a documents call: with no maintenance covenant and no junior class, the risk is not a default, it is an *exchange* — a non-pro-rata offer that moves some pari passu holders ahead of others, or a drop-down of Inovyn or the Sinopec JVs into unrestricted subsidiaries. A desk buys the notes only after reading the incurrence baskets the blog quotes and the annual report does not [S2][S14]. The callable-at-102 feature is irrelevant at 80 but matters if the company ever gets to par.
- **2029 term loans (€2,793 million).** The biggest block and the most exposed: same rung as the notes, floating rate (so the coupon rises with EURIBOR/SOFR), held by CLOs whose rules constrain their ability to take equity or to fund new money. In an LME the term-loan class is where the cooperation agreements form, because it is where the money is. A desk that wants control accumulates here; a desk that wants to be paid to wait prefers the fixed-coupon notes.
- **2030 notes (€675 million, 6.75%).** The lowest coupon and longest tenor among the notes, so the lowest price (70–78) and the highest yield (17%) [S14][S15]. Same rung as everything else, so the discount to the 2029s is a curve view — that 2030 is after the wall — not a ranking view. A desk that believes the 2029 wall gets refinanced buys the 2030s for the extra ten points of convexity; a desk that believes in an LME prefers the 2029s, which mature first and would anchor any exchange.
- **The Goldman inventory facility and the securitisations.** Not investable, but the structural read: about €1 billion of capacity ranks ahead of the notes on the most liquid assets without a lien, because it is a *sale*, not a loan. Every euro drawn reduces what the secured class would recover in a liquidation. Watch the drawn balance each quarter: €190.6 million at June [S1].
- **The equity basket.** €200 million of a chemicals company's cash in other chemicals companies' shares is a leveraged bet on the same cycle the bonds are betting on, funded with the lenders' collateral cash and marked down €54.9 million in its first quarter [S1]. A creditor reads it as (a) the owner's confidence, (b) €200 million that did not buy back 2030 notes at 78 — which would have retired €256 million of face — and (c) a test of the investments covenant [S26].
- **The equity itself.** Ratcliffe's €200 million shows the owner's reservation price for keeping control is high, and 2009 shows he would rather pay banks a fee than hand them the keys [S9]. A desk models the owner's next move as: more equity, then asset sales, then an amend-and-extend with a fee — an exchange or a filing only if the cycle refuses to turn before 2029.

## 11. Risks and what could derail it

- **The cycle does not turn.** Q2 2026 was a supply-shock quarter with €70 million of inventory gains; Inovyn spreads reversed in June and Acetyls' Asian prices declined after an April spike [S3]. If EBITDA reverts toward the FY2025 run-rate of ~€700 million, leverage goes back to 7–8x and the 2029 refinancing is a distressed exchange by another name.
- **Market access.** Quattro has not issued in 2026; the sibling has [S7][S8]. Refinancing €3.9 billion in 2029 at 2026 yields of 16–17% would add roughly €300 million a year of interest to a €558 million bill [S1][S2][S14].
- **Structural layering and LME risk.** No maintenance covenants, an empty second-lien class under the intercreditor, unrestricted subsidiaries holding the Sinopec JVs, and blog-reported baskets for incremental debt and unrestricted-subsidiary transfers [S2][S14]. The tools for a Saks- or NFE-style priming are on the shelf; only the owner's choice and the baskets' true size stand in the way.
- **Asset sales that leak value.** The Esseco sale is undisclosed; a larger Inovyn sale would remove the "highest-quality holding" from the collateral, and the term loans' 100% sweep is "subject to reinvestment rights" [S2][S14][S24].
- **Cash spent outside the capital structure.** €400 million into listed peers by INEOS and its shareholders, €200 million of it from Quattro's own balance sheet [S1][S26].
- **Energy and policy.** Tavaux lost its ARENH nuclear pricing at end-2025 and now buys ~70% of its power from the Exeltium consortium at higher prices through 2029 [S2]; EU anti-dumping duties are an upside, not a base case [S14][S21].
- **The owner's other calls on cash.** INEOS Group Holdings carries €12.6 billion of net debt and Project One in Antwerp; Manchester United; Grangemouth's exit [S7][S13][S22]. Shareholder support is discretionary and the annual report says so [S2].
- **What happens if it fails.** With one pari passu class, no junior debt and no US filing history, the likeliest paths are an amend-and-extend with fees (the 2009 template), a non-pro-rata exchange, or an English scheme or Part 26A plan of the kind New Fortress used — the group's parent is English-registered [S2]. Not sourced: any adviser engagement, cooperation agreement or forbearance.

## 12. Concepts to teach in this episode

1. **Stressed versus distressed: the maturity wall and the refinancing question.** Definition: a stressed credit pays its coupons today but the market doubts it can refinance a future maturity; distress is when the price says the debt will not be paid in full. Why: this is the first episode where nothing has happened yet, and the desk's job is to say what will. Fact: €697 million due January 2027 against €1,576 million of cash versus €3,918 million due March 2029; bonds at 70–80 and 16–17% yields versus coupons of 6.75–9.625% [S1][S14]. Moyer: causes of distress; how distressed claims trade. Ledger: `liquidity`, `refinancing`, `price_in_points` are `introduced`; new id `maturity_wall`.
2. **One class of secured debt: pari passu notes and term loans under an intercreditor, with no maintenance covenants.** Definition: bonds and bank loans sharing one lien and one payment rank, so the lenders differ in documents, not in priority. Why: it decides who the fulcrum would be and why an LME is the risk rather than a default. Fact: €6.8 billion of "Pari Passu Debt" under the 2014/2020 intercreditor; the €500 million unsecured notes repaid January 2025; "no financial maintenance covenants" [S1][S2]. Moyer: capital structure and where value breaks; the fulcrum. Ledger: `intercreditor_agreement`, `indenture`, `credit_agreement`, `lien`, `covenant`, `fulcrum_security` `introduced`; new ids `pari_passu`, `covenant_lite`.
3. **Money that ranks ahead without a lien: securitisation, inventory monetisation and unrestricted subsidiaries.** Definition: selling receivables or inventory to a financier (a true sale) or moving assets into a subsidiary the covenants do not bind puts value outside the secured lenders' reach. Why: the €500 million package was half this. Fact: €300 million Goldman facility with buy-back "at the same price", €790 million receivables capacity, Sinopec JVs in unrestricted subsidiaries [S1][S2]. Moyer: structural subordination; guarantees (extended to modern true-sale and unrestricted-sub structures). Ledger: `structural_subordination`, `spv`, `drop_down`, `collateral_pools` `introduced`; new ids `securitisation`, `inventory_monetisation`, `unrestricted_subsidiary`.
4. **The owner's cheque: shareholder support, restricted payments and the investments covenant.** Definition: equity is the one source of cash that primes nobody; covenants limit what can flow out (restricted payments) and what the company may do with cash (investments); a controlling owner may also buy the debt. Why: Ratcliffe put €200 million in and €400 million into peers, and the 2009 precedent shows how he negotiates. Fact: €200 million new shares March 2026; €199.7 million equity basket, €54.9 million loss; 2009 consent fee, margin increase and EURIBOR floor from 230 banks; the annual report's "repurchases of our debt" risk factor [S1][S2][S9][S26]. Moyer: out-of-court restructuring; the negotiated deal; management and shareholder incentives. Ledger: `consent_fee` `introduced`; new ids `restricted_payments`, `shareholder_support`, `covenant_waiver`.
5. **Valuing a cyclical: trough, LTM and mid-cycle EBITDA times a multiple, and where that puts the fulcrum.** Definition: enterprise value is EBITDA times a multiple; for a cyclical both inputs move, and the fulcrum moves with them. Why: the entire debate about Quattro is which EBITDA and which multiple. Fact: €717 million (2025), €883 million (LTM), €407 million (one quarter); 5x = €3.6 billion, 6.5x = €5.7 billion, 8x on €1.1–1.4 billion = €8.8–11 billion against €5.6 billion of net debt; €70 million of inventory gains in Q2 [S1][S3][S14]. Moyer: valuation of the reorganized firm; the fulcrum security. Ledger: `enterprise_value`, `ebitda`, `adjusted_ebitda`, `leverage` `introduced`; new ids `ev_ebitda_multiple`, `mid_cycle_ebitda`, `inventory_holding_gains`.
6. **Ratings, yields and short sellers: what a B3/B rating and a 16% yield say.** Definition: a rating is an agency's default probability bucket; a bond's yield to maturity is the return if it pays; the gap between coupon and yield is the market's doubt. Why: two downgrades moved this credit from "high yield" to "distressed" in three months. Fact: Moody's B2→B3 December 2025, S&P BB−→B February 2026 with a 6.5x line; 2029s 16%, 2030s 17%; 20% short interest in a sibling bond [S13][S14][S19]. Moyer: how distressed claims trade. Ledger: `price_in_points` `introduced`; new ids `credit_rating`, `yield_to_maturity`.

No `needs_reteach` concepts exist in the ledger as of this research. Supporting terms the script will define for the first time (new ledger ids, pick six to ten): `maturity_wall`, `pari_passu`, `covenant_lite`, `securitisation`, `inventory_monetisation`, `unrestricted_subsidiary`, `restricted_payments`, `shareholder_support`, `covenant_waiver`, `ev_ebitda_multiple`, `mid_cycle_ebitda`, `inventory_holding_gains`, `credit_rating`, `yield_to_maturity`, `amend_and_extend`.

## 13. Glossary

- **Amend and extend:** a lender-consented change to a loan that pushes out its maturity, usually for a fee and a higher margin.
- **Anti-dumping duty:** a tariff imposed after a finding that imports are sold below fair value.
- **Bullet:** a loan or bond repaid in one payment at maturity.
- **Call price / optional redemption:** the price at which an issuer may repay a bond early, stepping down to par over time.
- **Consent fee:** a payment to lenders for agreeing to a waiver or amendment.
- **Covenant-lite:** a loan or bond with incurrence covenants only, no financial maintenance tests.
- **Credit-spread adjustment (CSA):** the fixed add-on to SOFR that replaced LIBOR's built-in credit premium.
- **Distressed:** trading at a price that implies the debt will not be paid in full.
- **Enterprise value (EV):** the value of the whole business, equal to net debt plus equity value.
- **Excess cash-flow sweep:** a term-loan requirement to prepay a percentage of annual cash flow above a threshold.
- **Guarantor coverage test:** a requirement that guarantors represent a minimum share of group EBITDA and assets.
- **Incurrence covenant:** a limit tested only when the company acts (borrows, pays a dividend), not on a schedule.
- **Intercreditor agreement:** the contract among lender groups fixing lien and payment priority.
- **Inventory holding gain or loss:** the profit or loss from selling inventory bought at a lower or higher cost than current prices.
- **Inventory monetisation:** selling inventory to a financier with an agreement to buy it back, raising cash without a lien.
- **LTM:** last twelve months.
- **Maintenance covenant:** a financial ratio tested every quarter whose breach is a default.
- **Make-whole:** an early-redemption price that compensates the holder for lost coupons, here Bund plus 50 bp.
- **Maturity wall:** a large amount of debt maturing in one window.
- **Mid-cycle EBITDA:** the average earnings a cyclical business is expected to make across a full cycle.
- **Negative outlook:** an agency's signal that the next rating move is more likely down.
- **Pari passu:** ranking equally in right of payment and, here, sharing the same collateral.
- **Priming:** placing new debt ahead of existing lenders on the same collateral.
- **Restricted payments:** dividends, buybacks and other value leaving the restricted group, limited by covenant.
- **Restricted / unrestricted subsidiary:** a subsidiary bound / not bound by the debt covenants; unrestricted subsidiaries' assets are outside the lenders' reach.
- **Securitisation:** selling receivables into a vehicle that borrows against them.
- **Short interest:** the share of a bond issue borrowed and sold by investors betting its price falls.
- **Stressed:** paying today but with a doubted ability to refinance.
- **True sale:** a transfer of assets that a court would treat as a sale, not a secured loan, so the buyer owns them outright.
- **Yield to maturity:** the annual return from holding a bond to maturity at its current price if it pays in full.
