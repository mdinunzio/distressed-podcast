# Brightline: a $7.1 billion private railroad files its holding companies, keeps the trains out of court, and lets the bond insurer and the mutual funds write the plan
As of: 2026-10-05. Slug: brightline. ## 1. Situation summary and timeline

Brightline is the only privately owned intercity passenger railroad in the United States: a 235-mile Miami–Orlando line, six stations, ten diesel-electric trainsets, up to 36 one-way trains a day, conceived by Fortress co-founder Wes Edens in 2012 and carrying 3.1 million passengers in 2025 [S1][S2]. It was financed like a project: about $7.1 billion of funded debt stacked in layers of entities, each layer secured only by the equity of the layer below, with $2.2 billion of tax-exempt municipal bonds at the operating company, half of them insured by Assured Guaranty, and then four more layers of taxable notes, unrated municipal bonds, "commuter" bonds and holding-company loans above it [S1][S2]. The bond documents assumed 6.6 million passengers and nearly $700 million of revenue; 2025 delivered 3.1 million and $214 million [S23]. In 2026 the company deferred every coupon it could, drained its debt-service reserves on July 1, and ran out of extensions. On September 24, 2026, seventeen holding companies filed Chapter 11 in New Jersey under a restructuring support agreement with Assured and the big municipal mutual funds (Nuveen, First Eagle, Invesco, Nomura), while the railroad itself stayed out of court [S1][S3][S4]. The deal: $4.4 billion of municipal bonds keep their principal and defer interest; the insurer and the funds put in a $257.7 million DIP and $490 million of exit money and take 95% of the equity; the $1.1 billion of taxable notes and the holding-company loans share a 5% pool, later sweetened to at least 4.75% plus warrants to bring the hedge-fund holdouts in; Fortress's equity goes to zero [S1][S2][S11][S31]. Judge Mark Hall approved the DIP on September 29 over the objection of the holding-company term lender, CK Opportunities (Certares and Knighthead), which has sued since 2023 over a $260 million insider sale of Brightline West equity to an Edens-controlled vehicle [S1][S12]. Support stood at $4.6 billion of debt that evening [S12][S30].

## 2. Business overview and why it broke

Brightline Trains Florida LLC ("OpCo") owns or controls the whole 235-mile system — track, land, trains, stations, maintenance facilities — 195 miles of it shared with Florida East Coast Railway between Miami and Cocoa and 40 miles of dedicated track to Orlando International Airport [S7]. Revenue is tickets (long-distance Miami–Orlando at a $71–76 average fare, short-distance South Florida at $24–27) plus ancillary (food, parking, partnerships); 88% of riders are Florida residents [S7]. 2025: $214 million of revenue (+14%), 3.12 million riders (+13%), a train-level operating loss of $68 million after $140.3 million of depreciation, an operating loss of $127 million, net interest of $114.6 million, a net loss of $233.1 million, operating cash outflow of $133.5 million and capex of $93.3 million [S2]. First half 2026: 1.79 million riders (+16%), $124.0 million of revenue (+17%), with June helped by four FIFA matches near the Aventura station [S7]. Trailing twelve months to July 31, 2026: 3.4 million riders, $234.3 million [S2].

**Causes of distress, in Moyer's categories.** This is a *project-finance ramp-up failure*, not an LBO: (1) **Demand assumed, not delivered.** The 2024 bond projections assumed 6.6 million passengers and $697 million of revenue (Debtwire's figure for the 2025 expectation is $633 million; the 2026 target was $795 million of revenue and $555 million of operating profit); actual 2025 ridership ran 53% below and revenue 69% below plan, and Q1 2026 monthly ridership of ~300,000 was 54% below the 2026 projection of 650,000 a month [S18][S23]. Edens's own framing of the market, quoted by Commercial Observer: "If we capture 4 percent of them, we make $200 million… 6 percent… $300 million, $400 million" [S23]. (2) **A capital structure sized to the projection.** Annual senior debt service alone is about $117 million; total annual debt payments about $162 million; from 2017 to 2025 the company paid $762 million of interest on $562 million of cumulative revenue [S21][S24]. Fitch: "The ramp-up has been difficult and is comparatively longer for Brightline than for other new transportation development projects" [S27]. (3) **Layering.** Each refinancing (2019 bonds for Orlando, the 2022 Holdings loan, the 2024 recap, the 2025 commuter remarketing) added a holding company with its own debt secured only by the equity below it, so that by 2026 there were five layers of lenders with different collateral, different documents and different incentives — the condition Debtwire calls "split along creditor lines" [S19]. (4) **Liquidity events on a calendar.** Coupon deferral options (three per series), grace periods and reserve funds bought exactly eighteen months: the first AAFOH deferral in July 2025, the second in January 2026, the February commuter default, reserves drained July 1, 2026, the "USD 1.5bn cliff" of June–July 2026, and a filing in September [S15][S16][S18]. (5) **Governance.** A sponsor-controlled board, an insider transaction in 2022 that a lender calls a fraudulent transfer, and a $115.7 million Virgin judgment on a trademark licence add the Moyer "mismanagement or conflicts" strand [S1][S2].

**Creditor's caveats.** There is no EBITDA line to lean on; the desk works from revenue growth (17%) against a debt service of $162 million that revenue of $234 million cannot carry after $142 million of cash operating costs — the company is "barely breaking even" at the operating line before any interest (Bond Buyer) [S11]. The ratio that matters is debt-service coverage, and OpCo's is below 1x on its own senior bonds, which is why reserve funds and deferrals, not cash flow, paid 2026's coupons [S20]. The tax-exempt status of the bonds depends on the project continuing to qualify, which is one reason the plan keeps the municipal principal intact and the issuer (FDFC) in place [S2]. Personal-injury claims (209 fatalities on the line as of May 2026, mostly at crossings) rank as general unsecured claims at OpCo, ahead of nothing but equity, but Bond Buyer notes they complicate any OpCo filing [S16][S18]. And the only entity likely to count as a "railroad" under the Bankruptcy Code is OpCo, which, if it ever filed, would be in Subchapter IV with a mandatory trustee — a second reason to keep it out [S19][S20].

## 4. Capital structure, senior to junior

The structure is best read as a tower of entities. Bottom to top: **OpCo** (Brightline Trains Florida LLC, non-debtor, owns the railroad) → **BLTF Holdings** (debtor, owns 100% of OpCo, pledged to the OpCo bonds) → **Brightline East LLC** (debtor, issuer of the taxable notes, secured by its equity in BLTF Holdings) → **AAF Operations Holdings LLC** ("AAFOH", non-debtor, issuer of the HoldCo bonds, secured by its membership interests in Brightline East and Brightline Tampa) → **Brightline Florida Holdings LLC** (non-debtor, borrower of the commuter bonds) → **Brightline Holdings LLC** (debtor, borrower of the CK term loan, secured by equity in its subsidiaries including Brightline Management LLC, the employer) and its siblings **BLH Investment** (debtor; $775 million facility secured by Brightline West equity) and **BL Florida** (debtor; $112.6 million facility). Fortress's funds sit above all of it [S1][S2][S19].

**Structural points.** OpCo's lenders — the $2.2 billion of bonds, the NPA notes and Citizens — hold the only lien on real assets: track, stations, trains, revenues. Every layer above holds a lien on *equity* of the layer below, which is worth whatever is left after that layer's debt: Brightline East on BLTF Holdings (so behind the OpCo bonds), AAFOH on Brightline East (behind the 11% notes), the commuter bonds on county rights that do not yet exist and a second lien on Brightline West, the Holdings loan on a management company and other subsidiaries [S1][S2][S19]. A default at Brightline East does not cross-default OpCo [S19]. Assured, as insurer of 50.1% of the OpCo bonds, controls their remedies and is "the key gatekeeper" [S2][S20]. The DIP is borrowed by the non-debtor OpCo but secured on debtor BLTF Holdings' pledge of OpCo's equity — a priming lien at the one place where the estate touches the railroad [S1][S2].

## 5. The key documents and what they say

- **The OpCo bond indenture (Series 2024 PABs, FDFC conduit issuer, Deutsche Bank trustee)** [S1][S2]: first lien on OpCo assets and a pledge of 100% of OpCo's equity by BLTF Holdings; Assured's policy covers 50.1% of principal and gives it the controlling vote; coupons January 1 and July 1; a debt-service reserve fund that paid January 1 and July 1, 2026. The RSA proposes **three coupon deferrals through January 2028, repaid January 1, 2031**, with a deferral fee, Assured guaranteeing the deferred interest for electing holders and continuing to guarantee scheduled interest for non-electing holders [S2][S4][S11].
- **The Brightline East indenture (11% notes due 2030)** [S1][S2][S8]: secured by Brightline East's equity in BLTF Holdings and personal property; a $48 million reserve; KBRA's "structural subordination to any existing and future debt issued by Brightline Florida". Under the RSA (as of September 25): "~$48M cash + 50%+ of the Junior Stakeholder Pool" [S2]; by September 30, at least 4.75% of the reorganised equity, warrants, governance rights and a right of first offer [S31].
- **The AAFOH indenture (Series 2024/2024A)** [S2][S7][S15]: three coupon deferral options with a 2% step-up; a third missed payment "= event of default" (Debtwire); amended four times in 2026 to extend grace periods; under the RSA, principal untouched, tax-exempt status preserved, cash-interest grace through the earlier of September 24, 2027 or five business days after RSA termination; AAFOH "separates" from the Brightline Florida structure without filing [S1][S2].
- **The commuter-bond indenture (Series 2025B; UMB successor trustee)** [S1][S2][S7][S15]: amended thirteen times in 2026 to extend the February 17 interest grace period (to September 28) and the June 15 mandatory tender; 2% step-up during grace; under the RSA, principal untouched, second lien on Brightline West equity preserved.
- **The Brightline Holdings credit agreement (June 2022; Morgan Stanley agent)** [S1][S2][S34][S35]: 20% PIK, $484 million, September 2027; a make-whole; negative covenants that CK says the December 2022 Brightline West sale breached; the "Subsidiaries" definition that Brightline and Morgan Stanley read as excluding Brightline West after that sale, releasing guarantees, collateral and prepayment obligations. Under the RSA, the lender receives "100% of the equity of Brightline Holdings LLC's pledged subsidiaries" plus a share of the pool.
- **The RSA (September 25, 2026)** [S1][S2][S3][S4]: parties — the company, Assured, the Ad Hoc Mutual Fund Group; "Required Supporting Holders" = Assured plus group members holding ≥75% of commitments. Implementation by a Chapter 11 plan for BLTF Holdings (possibly joint) *or* a §363 sale of OpCo's equity if the supporting holders elect. Exit: $140 million senior notes pari passu with the OpCo bonds (Assured $70 million or insurance; the funds backstop $70 million; search excerpt: 8.5% PIK) and $350 million second-lien junior notes (mutual-fund purchasers, 10% PIK backstop fee; other consenting OpCo holders and Parent Noteholders may take an allocation). New HoldCo takes OpCo's equity: 95% to the junior-note purchasers, 5% to a Junior Stakeholder Pool for debtor creditors, both diluted up to 10% by a management incentive plan. Intercompany contracts released; past-service claims waived. Milestones: plan/DS 60 days, DS order 105 days, confirmation 45 days after solicitation, effectiveness 14 days after; outside date 315/365 days.
- **The DIP note purchase agreement (interim order September 29)** [S1][S2][S12]: $257.7 million, borrower non-debtor OpCo, purchasers Assured (backstop to $178 million), the mutual-fund group and consenting OpCo holders ratably; 10% PIK, 8% commitment premium, backstop premium "fully earned at interim order"; no conventional roll-up but $104.2 million repays the OpCo NPA notes; priming lien on BLTF's OpCo equity; adequate protection to primed OpCo secured parties by replacement liens, §507(b) claims, current payment of Assured's premiums, non-default interest and fees; carve-out capped at $7.5 million post-trigger; events of default include milestones, change of control, a trustee or examiner "excluding §1163 railroad trustee"; use of proceeds 97% operations, 3% case costs (CK's complaint).
- **Not public**: the plan, the disclosure statement, the full RSA term sheet, and the intercreditor terms between the new senior notes, the DIP and the OpCo bonds.

## 6. Where the fulcrum security sits and why

No valuation is public; the fulcrum has to be read from prices, ratings and the deal. Octus's June read: value "breaks inside the senior OpCo bonds" [S20]. The prices agree: uninsured OpCo bonds at 57–70 in 2026 (64 on September 3); the Brightline East notes at 8.5–10; the AAFOH bonds at 33–37; the commuter bonds at 63 (a price that reflects their Brightline West and Tampa collateral rather than OpCo) [S11][S14][S18][S21]. The arithmetic behind it: annual senior debt service of ~$117 million against a railroad whose revenue is $234 million and whose cash operating costs run ~$142 million [S2][S21][S24] — OpCo cannot cover its own senior coupons, so the senior bonds themselves are impaired on cash flow and the enterprise is worth less than $2.2 billion on any coverage basis the market will underwrite today. That makes the **OpCo bonds the fulcrum**, and it explains the deal's shape: the people who own the fulcrum (Assured and the mutual funds) write the plan, keep their principal, defer their coupons, and take 95% of the equity through the new junior notes they buy; everyone structurally above them — $1.1 billion of 11% notes, $1.2 billion of HoldCo bonds, $985 million of commuter bonds, $1.37 billion of holding-company loans — is out of the money on OpCo, which is why the plan can offer the corporate notes a 5% pool and the HoldCo and commuter bonds "principal untouched" that is mostly a claim on a *different* set of assets (Tampa rights, Brightline West equity, county agreements) than the railroad [S1][S2][S31]. Moyer's test — the class where enterprise value runs out — lands on the senior municipal bonds, with the twist that half of that class is insured, so the insurer, not the bondholder, bears the loss and holds the pen. Who holds it: Assured ($1.13 billion insured), First Eagle and Nuveen (~$1 billion each per Private Equity Wire; two fund complexes held 39% of the municipal bonds and the top four 56% per Bond Buyer), Invesco, Nomura, BlackRock [S11][S14][S33].

## 7. The restructuring, step by step

1. **Buy time with the documents (Jul 2025 – Sep 2026).** Use all three AAFOH deferral options; let the commuter interest go unpaid with a 2% step-up and amend the indenture thirteen times; draw the $45 million revolver; pay January and July OpCo coupons from the DSRF; sell $43 million (later $149 million) of OpCo notes at 70 to Assured [S7][S15][S16][S18].
2. **Pick the DIP provider (Aug–Sep 2026).** Three factions compete; Assured commits $350 million "in case of bankruptcy"; the municipal funds, who hold the fulcrum, win; the hedge funds (Davis Polk) are left outside the RSA [S22][S30].
3. **File the holding companies, not the railroad (Sep 24).** Seventeen debtors, ~$2.5 billion of debtor-level debt; OpCo, AAFOH and Brightline Florida Holdings stay out, which keeps the tax-exempt bonds and their collateral "outside Ch. 11" (Miller) and avoids Subchapter IV [S1][S11][S19].
4. **DIP through the back door (Sep 29).** $257.7 million borrowed by the non-debtor OpCo, secured by a priming lien on the *debtor's* pledge of OpCo equity, with the debtors as capped guarantors; interim $185–190 million approved over CK's objection [S1][S2][S12].
5. **Exit financing and equity (at effectiveness).** $140 million senior notes pari passu with the OpCo bonds; $350 million second-lien junior notes whose buyers receive 95% of New HoldCo; OpCo coupons deferred three times to January 2028 and repaid in 2031; AAFOH and commuter principal untouched and those entities separated without filing [S1][S2][S4].
6. **Cram the top of the tower (plan).** Brightline East notes: ~$48 million of reserve cash plus most of a 5% pool, raised on September 30 to "at least 4.75%" of the equity plus warrants, governance and a ROFO; the CK loan: 100% of the pledged subsidiaries' equity (including the employer) plus a pool share; BLHI: keeps its Brightline West lien plus a pool share; BL Florida: a 40% Las Vegas property interest plus a pool share; Fortress's equity: zero [S1][S2][S21][S31].
7. **Milestones**: plan by ~November 23, DS order by ~January 7, 2027, confirmation ~45 days later, effectiveness 14 days after; §363 sale of OpCo's equity as the alternative path if the supporting holders elect [S1][S2].

**Where the value accumulated.** At OpCo, in the hands of the people who already held the fulcrum: the senior municipal bonds keep every dollar of principal, and the $350 million of new junior notes — bought by the same mutual funds, backstopped for a 10% fee — carries 95% of the equity of the reorganised railroad. The insurer keeps its policy in force, lends $178 million of the DIP and $70 million of the exit, and has not yet paid a claim. Everything above OpCo was converted into claims on *other* assets — Tampa extension rights, Brightline West equity, county commuter agreements, a 40% Las Vegas property — plus a 5% pool. The railroad's value stayed in the railroad; the tower above it was unwound.

**Twists, turns and risks.** December 2022: the Brightline West sale that removed the growth project from the Holdings lenders' collateral and started a three-year lawsuit that is now a DIP objection. July 1, 2026: the reserve-funded coupons that postponed default "for the final time". August 2026: Assured's $350 million super-senior commitment, which decided who would be the DIP lender. September 29: the East Noteholders and the Cleary group signing "minutes ahead" of the hearing, taking support from $3.6 billion to $4.6 billion; the next day the 4.75% equity sweetener. The desk was watching one thing: which faction would fund the DIP, because the DIP lender writes the plan.

**Winners, losers, and who was left in the dust.** Winners: Assured and the municipal funds (par principal, the new junior notes, 95% of the equity, the fees); the trains and the 520 employees. Losers: the Brightline East noteholders, from $1.119 billion of 11% secured notes to cash of ~$48 million and a few percent of equity — the hedge funds that bought at 10 cents may still do fine; the original holders do not. Left in the dust: Fortress's equity at zero after fourteen years; the AAFOH and commuter bondholders, whose "untouched" principal is now a claim on expansions that have no construction date; and CK Opportunities, which gets a management company and a lawsuit.

## 10. How a special situations desk would look at each part of the structure

- **Insured OpCo bonds (par).** Not a distressed trade; a bet on Assured's claims-paying ability. The only question is whether to elect the deferral (Assured guarantees it) or keep scheduled interest (also guaranteed) [S4].
- **Uninsured OpCo bonds (57–70).** The fulcrum in municipal clothing: par principal, coupons deferred to 2028 and repaid in 2031, now sitting behind a $257.7 million DIP and $140 million of new senior notes and beside $350 million of second-lien junior notes. At 64 you are buying a 5.25% tax-exempt coupon with a three-year holiday on a railroad growing revenue 17% a year; the trade is the ramp-up, and the risk is the next deferral [S2][S14].
- **The new junior notes / New HoldCo equity.** The control position, available only to RSA parties and allocated by backstop; 95% of the equity for $350 million against a ~$2.2 billion senior stack says the plan values the equity cheaply — the sponsor's option of episode 4, now held by the lenders.
- **Brightline East 11% notes (8.5–10).** Bought at those prices by Redwood, Aristeia, Nut Tree and Phoenix, the notes were an option on a seat at the table; the September 30 sweetener (≥4.75% equity, warrants, governance, ROFO) is what the option paid. The original par holders lost ~90% [S18][S21][S31].
- **AAFOH bonds (33–37) and commuter bonds (63).** "Principal untouched" but no longer railroad claims: Tampa rights that need $400 million of debt and have no start date, county agreements not yet signed, a second lien on Brightline West equity that depends on a $6 billion RRIF loan and a missed $400 million equity deadline. Price them as options on expansions, not as railroad bonds [S2][S15][S18][S41].
- **The CK loan.** A litigation claim: the $750 million make-whole case, the fraudulent-transfer theory on Brightline West, equitable subordination of Fortress-related claims — plus ownership of the company that employs the workforce the railroad needs [S1][S2].
- **Fortress / Brightline West.** Zero at Brightline Florida; the real exposure is Brightline West, where a $21.5 billion project awaits a $6 billion federal loan and a $400 million equity cheque now due November 2, 2026 [S21][S25][S41].

## 11. Risks and what could derail it

1. **Confirmation.** The plan is not filed; CK can object to classification, releases, the Brightline West claims and the treatment of Brightline Management; the East Noteholders' deal is a term sheet, not votes [S1][S31].
2. **The 2022 transaction.** A summary-judgment ruling for CK in New York, or an estate fraudulent-transfer claim, could pull Brightline West value back toward the Holdings lenders and complicate releases [S1][S2].
3. **January 1, 2027.** If the plan slips, the next OpCo and Brightline East coupons fall due with reserves empty; the DIP is sized for operations (97%) and matures at the earlier of one year or effectiveness [S1][S15].
4. **Ridership.** Revenue is growing 17% but remains a third of the 2024 projection; Columbia's Sabourin: the improvement "doesn't seem enough to support that kind of debt load" [S11].
5. **Railroad-specific law.** A §1163 trustee if OpCo ever files; Railroad Retirement Board back-contributions since 2019; FECR arbitration over commuter rights; 209 crossing and trespasser fatalities and the injury claims that follow [S2][S18][S19].
6. **Tax-exempt status and the FDFC** if the structure changes; the HoldCo and commuter "separations" have to close without a filing [S2].
7. **Assured's own position.** Surveillance category 3 would mean paying claims; losses so far sit inside deferred premium, which changes if deferrals become defaults [S6].
8. **Brightline West contagion.** A missed equity deadline or a denied RRIF loan removes the only collateral behind the commuter bonds' second lien and the BLHI facility [S25][S41].

## 12. Concepts to teach in this episode

New (to add to the ledger as `introduced`, episode 6):

- `bond_insurance` (level 3, capital_structure): a monoline insurer guarantees scheduled principal and interest, so the insured bonds trade at par while the uninsured half of the same issue trades at 60, and the insurer takes the controlling vote and the loss; Assured's $1.13 billion, "I don't mind owning a railroad", $44 million of loss development. Moyer: who really holds the claim.
- `private_activity_bonds` (level 3, capital_structure): tax-exempt municipal bonds issued through a conduit (FDFC) for a private project, bought by municipal mutual funds whose mandate is tax-exempt income and par preservation; why the plan leaves $4.4 billion of principal untouched and defers coupons instead. Not in Moyer by name.
- `holdco_opco_layering` (level 3, capital_structure): a tower of entities in which each layer's debt is secured only by the equity of the layer below, so "secured" claims above the operating company are structurally subordinated to everything at it; Brightline East on BLTF Holdings, AAFOH on Brightline East, the commuter bonds on county rights. Moyer: capital structure and where value breaks.
- `ramp_up_risk` (level 3, valuation): project-finance demand risk — debt sized to a ridership forecast (6.6 million) against delivery (3.1 million), coverage below 1x, reserves and deferrals as the bridge. Moyer: causes of distress.
- `debt_service_reserve_fund` (level 2, accounting): a cash reserve pledged to pay coupons when operations cannot; drawn January 1 and July 1, 2026; its depletion is the clock. Not in Moyer by name.
- `coupon_deferral_option` (level 3, legal): indenture rights to defer a fixed number of coupons with a step-up and to extend grace periods by amendment; three AAFOH deferrals, 2% step-ups, thirteen commuter amendments; the mechanics of "amend and extend" without a filing. Moyer: out-of-court restructuring.
- `non_debtor_opco` (level 4, process): filing the holding companies and keeping the operating company out, with the DIP borrowed by the non-debtor and secured by a priming lien on its equity held by a debtor; the reasons (tax-exempt collateral, §1163 railroad trustee, personal-injury claims, operations). Moyer: Chapter 11 process, DIP financing.
- `make_whole` (level 3, legal): the prepayment premium that compensates lenders for lost yield, and how it becomes a $750 million claim when a borrower restructures around it; CK's case against Morgan Stanley. Not in Moyer by name.
- `equitable_subordination` (level 4, legal): a bankruptcy court's power to push an insider's or wrongdoer's claim below others; one of CK's reserved estate claims against the Edens-related entities. Moyer: avoidance and subordination.

## 13. Glossary

- **AAFOH**: AAF Operations Holdings LLC, issuer of the $1.2 billion HoldCo bonds.
- **Adequate protection**: what a primed secured lender must receive (replacement liens, superpriority claims, payments) for a court to approve a DIP ahead of it.
- **Bond insurance / monoline wrap**: a financial guarantee of scheduled bond payments by an insurer such as Assured Guaranty.
- **Carve-out**: the slice of DIP collateral reserved for professional fees and US Trustee fees.
- **Commuter bonds**: the Series 2025B bonds secured by future county commuter-rail agreements and a second lien on Brightline West equity.
- **Conduit issuer**: a governmental issuer (the Florida Development Finance Corporation) that issues tax-exempt bonds on behalf of a private borrower.
- **Coupon deferral option**: an indenture right to postpone a set number of interest payments, usually with a step-up in rate.
- **Debt-service reserve fund (DSRF)**: cash held by the trustee to pay debt service if revenues fall short.
- **DIP**: debtor-in-possession financing, here borrowed by a non-debtor and guaranteed by the debtors.
- **Equitable subordination**: a court's power under §510(c) to subordinate a claim because of the claimant's inequitable conduct.
- **Junior Stakeholder Pool**: the 5% of New HoldCo equity reserved under the RSA for creditors of the debtor entities.
- **Make-whole**: a prepayment premium equal to the lender's lost future interest.
- **Mandatory tender**: a date on which bondholders must tender bonds for purchase, typically funded by a remarketing; failure is a default.
- **Non-debtor affiliate**: an entity in the group that has not filed and is not protected by the automatic stay.
- **OpCo**: Brightline Trains Florida LLC, the operating railroad.
- **Private activity bond (PAB)**: a tax-exempt municipal bond whose proceeds fund a private project.
- **Priming lien**: a new lien placed ahead of existing liens on the same collateral, with court approval.
- **Ramp-up**: the period after a project opens during which demand builds toward forecast.
- **Right of first offer (ROFO)**: the right to be offered a deal before third parties.
- **Section 1163 trustee**: the trustee that must be appointed in a railroad reorganisation under Subchapter IV of Chapter 11.
- **Step-up rate**: the higher interest rate that applies while a payment is deferred or in grace.
- **Structural subordination**: a creditor of a parent ranks behind every creditor of the subsidiary that owns the assets.
