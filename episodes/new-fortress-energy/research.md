## 1. Situation summary and timeline

New Fortress Energy Inc. ("NFE", Nasdaq: NFE) builds and operates LNG
import terminals, gas-fired power and a floating liquefaction unit,
mainly in Puerto Rico, Mexico, Nicaragua and Brazil, with a fleet of
chartered vessels [S4]. It grew by borrowing: about $8.3 billion of
consolidated debt at December 31, 2025 against 2025 revenue of $1.5
billion and a net loss of $1.8 billion [S4]. A 2024 exchange that
created $2.73 billion of 12% secured notes at a new subsidiary bought
time but not solvency [S9][S10][S4]. In 2025 revenue fell 36%, the
company missed a $163.8 million coupon on the 12% notes in November and
term loan interest in December, and entered forbearance [S4][S11][S12].
On March 17, 2026 it signed an RSA with holders of about 97% of $5.7
billion of corporate debt [S1][S15]. Instead of Chapter 11, two
English-incorporated subsidiaries proposed Restructuring Plans under Part
26A of the Companies Act 2006, sanctioned June 18, 2026 and recognized in
New York under Chapter 15 on June 26 (order June 29) [S6][S15][S16].
On September 11, 2026 the deal closed: the Brazil business was spun to
creditors as "BrazilCo"; the rest ("CoreCo", still NFE) exchanged $5.7
billion of debt for $571 million of take-back term loans, $2.46 billion
of mandatorily convertible preferred stock and 65% of the common, with
existing shareholders keeping 35% [S6]. That equity retention is the
headline: it would not have survived the absolute priority rule in a
Chapter 11 cramdown [S15][S17].

## 2. Business overview and why it broke

**What it does.** Two segments: Terminals and Infrastructure (LNG import,
regasification, gas-fired power, gas sales in Puerto Rico, Mexico,
Nicaragua and, until closing, Brazil) and Ships (chartered LNG carriers
and FSRUs, largely through the Energos sale-leaseback) [S4]. It also owns
"Fast LNG 1", a floating liquefaction unit off Altamira, Mexico, and an
unfinished FLNG 2 [S4][S6]. The model: build a terminal and power plant in
a market short of gas, sign a long-term supply contract, and buy LNG to
fill it.

**Moyer-style causes of distress.**
1. *Capital intensity funded by debt.* Consolidated debt went from about
   $8.9 billion at end-2024 to $8.3 billion at end-2025 [S4], across
   corporate notes and loans, a $635 million vessel sale-leaseback, Brazil
   project debt ($849 million PortoCem debentures, $377 million BNDES,
   $386 million Brazil notes) and turbine financing. Interest expense was
   $778 million in 2025 against $340 million of segment operating margin
   [S4].
2. *Revenue collapse.* 2025 revenue of $1.50 billion versus $2.36 billion
   in 2024; segment operating margin down 69% [S4]. The 2024 figure had
   included $150 million of "deferred earnings from contracted sales" and
   the emergency-power business in Puerto Rico that ended in March 2024
   [S4].
3. *Project and contract setbacks.* The FEMA-funded Puerto Rico emergency
   power contract was terminated in March 2024 [S4]; Puerto Rico rejected
   a $20 billion, 15-year supply proposal in July 2025 before agreeing a
   smaller 7-year deal (press) [S21]; Brazilian power auctions were
   delayed and Nicaragua's COD slipped to 2027 [S7][S15]; FLNG 2 needs
   $750 million to $1.5 billion more [S4].
4. *Liquidity, not just leverage.* Liquidity was ~$80 million in
   September 2024 [S9]; the 2024 refinancing "addressed near-term
   maturities but did not resolve the group's underlying difficulties"
   [S17]; by November 2025 the company could not pay a $163.8 million
   coupon [S11].
5. *Accounting problems.* The 2023 and 2024 statements were restated and
   material weaknesses identified [S4].

**Sequence.** Growth capex on borrowed money (2021-2023) → contract loss
and liquidity squeeze (2024) → $400 million equity raise and $2.73
billion 12% secured exchange at a new subsidiary (Oct-Nov 2024) → asset
sales (Jamaica, turbines) and downgrades (2025) → missed coupon and
forbearance (Nov-Dec 2025) → RSA (Mar 2026) → English plans and Chapter
15 (Apr-Jul 2026) → closing (Sept 11, 2026).

**Structural points.**
- The 2024 exchange created a *subsidiary issuer* (NFE Financing LLC)
  whose notes have first liens on assets that were *not* collateral for
  the revolver, LC facility and term loans, plus a pledge of up to 49% of
  the Brazil holding company, and "direct or indirect first priority
  secured claims against the remaining" business through intercompany
  loans [S10]. That is why the New 2029 Notes ended up with 94.3% of
  BrazilCo and the largest slice of the CoreCo preferred [S1].
- The plans allocated value in two steps: first among five collateral
  pools by the value uplift each pool contributed, then pro rata within
  each pool [S15][S17]. The legacy notes' pool (core collateral only)
  was the weakest: 26% recovery under the plans versus 13% in the
  relevant alternative [S15][S16].
- Two English subsidiaries were the plan companies. NFE Global Holdings
  Limited had guaranteed the CoreCo debt since 2021; NFE Brazil Newco
  Limited was incorporated in April 2026 and acceded as guarantor of the
  BrazilCo debt so the English court had jurisdiction ("sufficient
  connection" through incorporation) while the parent stayed out of any
  proceeding [S15][S16][S1].

## 6. Where the fulcrum security sits and why

Moyer's fulcrum is the senior-most impaired class that receives the
equity. Here the plans deliberately blurred it.

- *Value.* PJT's TEV and the explanatory statement's numbers were not
  fetched. What is public: creditors received consideration valued at
  $3.32 billion post-plan (Clifford Chance's reading of the expert
  evidence) against $5.7 billion of claims [S15]; the fair value of all
  debt on the balance sheet was $5.9 billion at June 30, 2026 [S5]; the
  cleansing case shows CoreCo AEBITDA of $451 million (or $326 million
  stressed) for 2027 [S7]. On those numbers the corporate debt was
  impaired by roughly 40%, so every class from the 12% notes down was
  the fulcrum in the Moyer sense.
- *Who got the equity.* 65% of CoreCo common to all plan creditors pro
  rata to their preferred allocation, plus 100% of BrazilCo mostly to the
  12% noteholders [S1]. The rest of the creditors' value sits in $2.46
  billion of preferred that converts into 87% of the common in three
  years unless redeemed [S1][S6]. So the *economic* fulcrum is the
  preferred: it is the instrument that decides whether old shareholders
  keep 35% or about 4.5% (35% of 13%).
- *The 35%.* Existing shareholders kept 35% of the common despite $5.7
  billion of debt being compromised. Every analyst source notes this
  would breach the absolute priority rule in a contested Chapter 11
  [S15][S17][S18]. It was possible because Part 26A has no APR, no class
  objected, and the parent needed Nasdaq and Delaware stockholder
  approvals to issue the new securities (the June 17 vote) [S23][S17].

## 7. The restructuring, step by step

**Step 1: the 2024 exchange (Sept-Nov 2024)** [S9][S10][S4][S24]
1. September 30: lenders grant a covenant holiday conditioned on
   refinancing the 2025 notes plus $300 million and raising $250 million
   of equity; liquidity ~$80 million.
2. October 1-2: 46.3 million shares sold at $8.63 (~$400 million); Edens
   buys 5.8 million.

   notes due 2029: $1,210 million for cash, $1,520 million exchanged
   dollar-for-dollar for 2026 and 2029 notes; $887 million repays the
   6.75% 2025 notes; supporting holders get a 5% fee in shares.

**Step 2: asset sales and default (2025)** [S4][S11][S12]
4. May 14: Jamaica sold for $678.5 million cash plus escrow.
5. November 17-20: $163.8 million coupon missed; forbearance.
6. December 10-31: TLB and TLA interest and TLB principal missed;
   forbearance to January 9.

**Step 3: RSA (Mar 17, 2026)** [S1]
7. Supporting creditors across every instrument; 0.75% early consent
   fee; standstill; separation into BrazilCo and CoreCo agreed.

**Step 4: English plans and Chapter 15 (Apr-Jul 2026)** [S14][S15][S16]
8. April 20 practice statement letter; May 14 convening order; May 28
   Chapter 15 petitions; June 9 voting deadline; June 15 meetings; June
   17 stockholder approvals; June 18 sanction; June 26/29 recognition.
9. June 19: BrazilCo raises $973.5 million of 12% PIK notes to stand on
   its own [S13].

**Step 5: closing (Sept 11, 2026)** [S6][S8]
10. Terminated debt exchanged per the allocation table; BrazilCo equity
    transferred to creditors; take-back loans, preferred and 65% common
    issued; $136.5 million new money; LC facility reset; reverse split;
    BrazilCo pays $74 million; Edens receives shares on his TLA and buys
    a small additional stake from creditors.

## 9. How a special situations desk would look at each part of the structure

- **The 12% notes.** The 2024 trade: put in $1.2 billion of new money at
  12% through a subsidiary issuer with a lien on the assets nobody else
  had, plus a 5% fee in stock. Even after the coupon default and a
  ~28-cent quote in January 2026 [S21], that pool delivered Brazil,
  preferred and common. The lesson is the same as in most modern
  restructurings: the class that writes the new money designs the
  collateral map that the next restructuring is allocated on.
- **Term Loan B.** Senior, its own pool, converted into the cash-pay
  take-back loans and FLNG 2 paper. The instrument to own if you wanted
  to be a lender rather than an owner after the deal.
- **Legacy notes.** Unsecured-like recourse to "common collateral" that
  every other lender also sat on: 26 cents. A desk buying these in 2025
  at 20 cents [S21] made money only because the plan paid more than the
  Chapter 11 alternative; the judge asked exactly that question [S16].
- **Preferred.** The instrument to model. $2.46 billion accreting at 3%,
  5%, 7%; redeemable at any time from enumerated proceeds (asset sales,
  FLNG 2 partner money, the FEMA settlement); mandatory conversion into
  87% of the common in September 2029. It is a three-year option for old
  equity to refinance $2.5 billion, and a near-certain equity transfer if
  they cannot [S1][S6].
- **Common.** 35% for old holders looks generous until the preferred is
  understood; the market price after the reverse split was not sourced.
- **The English route.** For a desk, the practical points: 75% by value
  per class with no numerosity test; no APR; releases of guarantors
  post-Purdue; no US filing by the listed parent, so no Nasdaq delisting
  and no Chapter 11 costs [S15]. Judge Glenn's guardrails (COMI scrutiny,
  section 1522(a), release carve-outs) are the checklist for the next one
  [S15][S16].

## 10. Risks and what could derail it

Closed, so remaining risks are execution:
- *Operating.* CoreCo AEBITDA of about $100 million in 2026 must reach
  $326-451 million in 2027 on Puerto Rico volumes, Mexico, market
  cargoes and Nicaragua's 2027 start; leverage 7.6x on 2026 numbers
  [S7]. FLNG 1 downtime directly reduces cargo sales [S7].
- *Liquidity.* $100 million minimum liquidity; ~$277 million cash to the
  balance sheet at close; $50 million undrawn junior accordion; the
  intermediation facility as the alternative [S7].
- *Preferred clock.* Conversion in September 2029 into 87% of the common
  unless redeemed [S1].
- *BrazilCo.* Now separate, with PortoCem obligations ($70 million
  contribution and $59.1 million guarantee) and Alunorte/Celba supply
  disputes [S5][S7]; CoreCo relies on ~$425 million turnover from the
  Brazil notes [S13].
- *Governance.* Interim CFO since September 3 [S22]; material weaknesses
  [S4]; a CEO who is also a creditor after buying TLA at a discount [S6].
- *Litigation.* FEMA/turbine and JPS arbitrations accrued [S4].

What could have derailed it: a dissenting class forcing cross-class
cramdown (none did); a Chapter 15 objection on COMI or public policy
(none filed); missing the September 15 outside date [S1].

## 11. Concepts to teach in this episode

1. **Causes of distress: capex-heavy growth on borrowed money.** Why: the
   whole structure was built to fund terminals and ships. Fact: $8.3
   billion of debt, $778 million of interest, $340 million of segment
   margin, revenue -36% [S4]. Moyer: causes of financial distress.
2. **Collateral pools and structural layering: the 2024 exchange.** Why:
   the subsidiary issuer with liens on "non-collateral" assets decided
   who got Brazil in 2026. Fact: $2.73 billion 12% notes, 49% Brazil
   pledge, intercompany claims; 94.3% of BrazilCo to that class [S10][S1].
   Moyer: capital structure and where value breaks; out-of-court
   exchanges (extended to modern drop-down / subsidiary-issuer
   structures).
3. **Restructuring support agreements and consent economics.** Why: 97%
   support was bought with fees and elections. Fact: 0.75% early consent
   fee, 2% standstill fee, $45 million cash election, preferred-to-debt
   election [S1]. Moyer: the negotiated plan; holdouts.
4. **Out-of-court versus in-court, and the English Part 26A plan with
   Chapter 15.** Why: this is the mechanism. Fact: two English plan
   companies, 75% threshold, six unanimous classes, "good forum
   shopping", Chapter 15 recognition with published guardrails [S15]-
   [S17]. Moyer: the Chapter 11 process, prepackaged plans; contrast.
5. **Absolute priority and the fulcrum: why equity kept 35%.** Why: the
   single most counter-intuitive fact. Fact: $5.7 billion → $571 million
   of loans plus $2.46 billion of preferred; 65/35 split; 87% conversion
   in three years; legacy notes at 26% [S1][S6][S15]. Moyer: absolute
   priority; the fulcrum security; valuation of the reorganized firm.
6. **Reading a post-restructuring balance sheet.** Why: "debt cut to $527
   million" hides $2.46 billion of accreting preferred, $400 million of
   non-recourse FLNG 2 debt, $635 million of vessel obligations and $2.0
   billion of consolidated long-term debt pro forma [S8][S6]. Moyer:
   valuation and capital structure after emergence.

## 12. Glossary

- **363 sale:** a sale of assets under section 363 of the Bankruptcy
  Code; the relevant alternative here assumed such sales.
- **Absolute priority rule:** in a contested Chapter 11 plan, a class
  must be paid in full before any junior class receives value.
- **Adjusted EBITDA ("AEBITDA"):** the cash-earnings measure used in the
  creditor materials; differs from GAAP and from segment operating
  margin.
- **Chapter 15:** the U.S. Bankruptcy Code chapter for recognizing and
  enforcing foreign insolvency and restructuring proceedings.
- **COMI:** centre of main interests; where a debtor's main proceeding
  belongs; England here, based on incorporation and administration.
- **Consent fee / standstill fee:** payments to creditors for signing the
  RSA early or for agreeing not to enforce.
- **Cross-class cramdown:** the Part 26A power to bind a dissenting class
  if it is no worse off than in the relevant alternative; not needed
  here.
- **Fulcrum security:** the class where enterprise value runs out; it
  receives the equity.
- **Liquidation preference:** the amount a preferred stock is entitled to
  before common receives anything.
- **Mandatorily convertible preferred:** preferred stock that converts
  into common on a fixed date unless redeemed.
- **Non-recourse:** debt whose lenders can look only to a specific asset
  pool, not the parent.
- **Part 26A Restructuring Plan:** the English court-supervised
  compromise introduced in 2020, with class voting at 75% by value and
  cross-class cramdown.
- **PIK:** interest or dividends paid by accretion rather than cash.
- **Relevant alternative:** the most likely outcome if the plan is not
  sanctioned; the benchmark for the "no worse off" test.
- **Restructuring support agreement (RSA):** the creditors' contract to
  support the plan on agreed terms and milestones.
- **Subsidiary issuer:** a new entity created to issue debt with its own
  collateral, used in 2024 to layer priority.
- **Sufficient connection:** the English test for jurisdiction over a
  plan company; incorporation in England satisfied it.
- **Take-back debt:** new loans issued under a plan to existing lenders.
- **Third-party release:** a release of claims against non-debtor
  guarantors; available in Part 26A, limited in Chapter 11 after Purdue.
- **Uplift:** the excess of plan recoveries over the relevant
  alternative; $1.44 billion here.
